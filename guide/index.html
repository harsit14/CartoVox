Handbook

How to use CartoVox, and where everything is.

Testers keep asking three questions: where do I find that, is it in there at all, and what does this setting actually do. This page answers all three. It starts with a world built from nothing in about a minute, then walks each workspace in the order you would meet it, and ends with a searchable index of every control and the exact place it lives.

Current as of the 0.9.0 beta, checked against a running copy. Screenshots are 0.9 and are of a real generated world. Version-by-version changes are on the release notes page; the field guide is the shorter inventory of what exists.

How to read a path

Bold is a label exactly as the app writes it. World › Tools › Turn world means: the World destination in the left rail, then the Tools menu in the dock along the bottom of the map, then Turn world inside it. If you are ever lost, press ⌘K / Ctrl K and type what you want — the command palette takes actions as well as names.

Start here

First launch

Two things stand between installing CartoVox and a world, and both are deliberate.

  • The licence. The first window is the End User License Agreement. Tick I have read and accept and Unlock becomes available. The same text stays readable at Settings › Updates & support › Licence.
  • The access key. Paste the key sent to you and choose Unlock CartoVox. Verification happens entirely on your computer: no account, no activation server, no hardware fingerprint. The key is personal to you and is stored in CartoVox's own application-data folder.
  • Then a six-panel tour — what the app simulates, how you describe a world, trying seeds, the three build sizes, drawing the atlas, and a finished world you can open on the spot. It appears once per version and can be closed at any panel. The manual did not go anywhere: it is under Help at the foot of the left rail.
  • Unsigned installers. macOS Gatekeeper and Windows SmartScreen will warn you, because the builds are not code-signed yet. Each release publishes checksums; install only the file shared in the beta Discord.
  • It makes no network request of its own. No update check, no telemetry, no account. The only address it opens is the loopback dashboard it started itself.
If you want to see a finished world first

Build the example world on the Create screen (and on an empty Library) builds Eldrebrenn from a fixed recipe: a complete project with data maps, illustrated plates, dossier, codex and gazetteer. A measured 83.9 seconds and 179 MB on a fast Apple Silicon Mac. It is a real project in your own library — open it, sculpt it, rename its realms, or throw it away. Nothing is built unless you press the button.

How the app is laid out

One window, five destinations, one record. Learn this and nothing is more than two clicks away.

Each workspace is a sheet on a desk

  • Create is one strip of actions above a folding brief, with the doors into import and drawing in the stage itself.
  • World is map-first: the rendered map is the page. A two-segment pill at the top left — World, Maps — opens the drawer beneath it. Everything that acts on the map is in the dock along the bottom: View, Export, Tools, the zoom controls, Focus map and fullscreen. The planet floats top right as its own card and can be put away; the colour key and the coordinate line sit bottom left.
  • Write is one strip above the page, with More and Book menus and a single panel switch.
  • Workspaces resume. World and Write reopen on the tab you last used. Settings › Workspace & layout can turn that memory off without erasing it.
  • A long build keeps running while you work elsewhere. The generation dock shows the phase, the plate and an ETA, and survives navigating away or reloading.

Tutorial

Your first world, start to finish

About a minute of computer time, on a machine that can run the app comfortably. Do this once and the rest of the handbook will make sense.

  1. Open Create. If the four doors are showing, choose Generate a new world, then a purpose (campaigning, climate study, an illustrated atlas, artwork, or writing). The purpose tunes which options are visible; it is not a preset. Skip — I know what I want goes straight to the sheet.
  2. Write two sentences in World thesis: what the world is, what is scarce or contested, and what the map must show. As you type, CartoVox reads it and offers each cue it recognised with its own Apply button — it never edits your brief for you. Use an example thesis writes a worked one for the silhouette you picked.
  3. Pick a shape. Fold 2 Planet & geography holds Planet type (Earth-like, Ice age, Lush and warm, Tilted world, The long day, The long winter, Great moon, Young and violent, Greenhouse ocean, Thin air, Dim star) and Geography (Balanced Earth-like, Pangaea, Shattered Archipelago, Inland Sea, Twin Hemispheres, Rifted Continent, Drowned Interior, Fjordland, Equatorial Belt). They are separate choices, so changing the kind of planet does not have to masquerade as changing its landmasses.
  4. Set Simulation detail to Sketch. A measured reference build finished in 30.5 seconds with an illustrated atlas on a fast Apple Silicon Mac. A Sketch is a real world, not a preview — every stage runs and the saved archive holds the same members.
  5. Press Preview this seed to see one candidate drawn in seconds, or Find 6 strong matches to score a batch of seeds against the brief. Keep the one you like.
  6. Read the two grey rows above the sheet before you commit: OUTPUT says what you will get, COST says how long and how much disk. The estimate learns from completed builds of the same shape on your own machine.
  7. Press the gold button. It names what you are committing to — Create Sketch world, Create Quick world, Create full-resolution world, or Generate publication atlas. The build shows you the world as it makes it: land and relief appear about seven seconds into a Quick build, and the map returns wearing its climate and biomes once rainfall and temperature have decided what grows where.
  8. Open it. The world lands in your Library and opens in World. Click a cell to ask it what it is. Open Maps to see the other fifty-odd views of the same geography. Open Atlas for the illustrated plates.
A Sketch can be promoted later

A coarser world can be rebuilt on a finer grid from World › World drawer › Rebuild at full simulation resolution. That starts a separate project from the same seed and recipe and leaves the original exactly as it was — coastlines, rivers, settlements, names and lore can all move on a finer grid, so the world you liked stays available as the fallback.

Making a world

Writing a brief the simulation can act on

The brief is five numbered folds. You can ignore all of them and press Generate; each one you fill in narrows what the search will accept.

FoldWhat it holds
1 Your storyThe World thesis and its live reading, and Story frame (epic world history, and other frames that shape the emphasis of generated lore).
2 Planet & geographyPlanet type, Geography, and Star, orbit & moon — day length, year length, stellar flux, lunar tide. Each fold states what it does not model.
3 Peoples & languagesNations, cultures and tongues — name realms and capitals before generation, request homeland traits, biomes, resources and trade goods, and drop a homeland pin with a visible search area. Language families blends real-language-inspired flavours by weight and previews a dozen names with glosses before the world exists.
4 World constraintsLand & sea, Climate & water, Peoples & magic — each a row of plain-language choices that summarise themselves in the fold's own header.
5 Tectonic intentThe Tectonic Director: craton nuclei, plate seeds, requested convergent/divergent/transform margins, drift bearings and speeds, and mantle hotspots. Partial intent is fine. The finished world reports which intentions were satisfied, compromised or impossible.
  • The thesis reading acts on words it knows and says so. Words it read but could not act on are reported back rather than dropped, and Which words does it know? prints its own vocabulary.
  • Hard and soft constraints are different things. A standalone continent count in Must have counts connected landmasses of at least 2 million km²; matches come before alternatives, misses state the actual count, and the saved world re-checks itself against the brief after simulation.
  • “Lush” means fewer deserts, not more rain. It weakens subtropical subsidence and strengthens inland moisture recycling. “Few deserts” in a thesis and “Avoid: deserts” pull the same lever.
  • The setup guide is optional. The strip shows Setup · n/6; Show guide walks the six steps and names which simulation systems each setting affects. Expert view puts it away.

Sketch, Quick, Full — and what a build costs

TierGridWhat it is for
SketchQuarter resolution (360 × 180)A whole world in about half a minute. Blocky coastlines, every stage run, the same archive members. A fast look at a seed you intend to keep.
QuickHalf resolutionCoarser coastlines, much faster. The usual working tier.
FullThe production gridWhat every published map is drawn from.
  • Every tier runs the same simulation. The difference is how many cells it was evaluated on, and nothing else — the codex, dossier, gazetteer, data maps and exports all work on a Sketch.
  • The illustrated atlas is most of the run time whichever tier you choose. Hand-drawn plates are the slow stage; the Create sheet says so above the button.
  • The preflight reports disk before it starts, for the chosen grid, finish and sheet set, and keeps a 1 GiB reserve. Current reference bands: 45–100 MiB for Quick, 180–360 MiB for Full without illustrated plates, 337–883 MiB for Full with every publication plate. CartoVox warns above 500 MiB.
  • Interrupted runs are detected and can be resumed from their stage checkpoint or removed. Stop after this plate finishes the current image cleanly and stores the rest.

Searching seeds, and choosing one

  • Preview this seed draws one candidate — approximate, nothing saved. Find 6 strong matches scores reproducible seeds against every measurable requirement. Search depth sets how many it tries: Quick 18 seeds, Thorough 36 (the default), Deep 72. Nothing is saved by a search.
  • Candidate cards keep Preview, Compare and More in one row and the consequential build button on its own. They distinguish checks that were measured from constraints that still need your judgement, and previews are labelled approximate because the production grid can change small islands.
  • Show 24 controlled variations offers structurally distinct alternatives of the same brief.
  • Favourite, reject and annotate. Favourites and rejections become an adjustable taste signal for later searches; hard constraints stay authoritative, and your notes never leave the browser.
  • Results survive. A run's settings are kept even when it fails or is cancelled, and Last generated is written automatically in a reserved recipe slot at the start of every run — so the settings behind a disappointing world are never lost.

Advanced settings, presets and recipes

Create › Advanced settings is the whole simulation: 306 inputs in twenty groups, in the order the simulation uses them — identity and grid, the planet (23), terrain import, tectonics (plates 9, continents 13, relief 66), presentation of the sphere, climate (temperature 11, circulation 8, rainfall 12, ocean 2), hydrology (44), surface geology and soils, glacial history, geological resources, biomes, the impossible layer (13), civilisation (29), naming (7) and output (31). Nothing is hidden in a module; every field carries a note and its allowed range.

  • Settings are bounded only where a value would break the simulation, not where it would merely make a strange world. A 62° tilt with tropical poles is reachable.
  • Recipes. Save as recipe keeps the preset plus only the settings you changed. Export recipe / Import from file moves one between machines; imports preview and ask before applying. Every generated world also writes a world recipe beside itself — seed, preset, changed settings, brief and thesis, nations, tectonic intent, output options and the imported or drawn foundation map.
  • Changing the world shape asks first — “Discard 3 changed settings?” — and Clear overrides says how many it will lose. The sheet survives a reload, and the Create strip carries the override count.

The illustrated atlas: finish, plates, lettering

  • Plate finish — Draft for faster review, Publication for full detail.
  • Atlas plates — choose World plate, Continents and Regions independently.
  • Atlas lettering — Labeled only, No labels, or Both versions from the same illustrated base. The missing variant can be added later without re-simulating.
  • Lake detail changes relief and illustrated drawing only — scientific views and lake data stay complete.
  • Deferred plates are not lost. World › Atlas › Render remaining plates draws them later from saved data, and Best-quality drawings redraws a Quick world's eligible maps at 4× from the exact saved simulation without changing any geography.

Making a world

Bringing in a map you already have

Import and Draw are one door — Create › Import/Draw — because they were always one room. CartoVox keeps your coastline and simulates everything else on top of it: climate, rivers, biomes, settlements, names and lore.

  1. Give it the file. PNG, JPEG, TIFF, numeric height grids, GeoJSON land outlines, shapefiles, GeoTIFFs, Azgaar exports, and OBJ or STL terrain surfaces. Limits scale with free disk and are reported before the import.
  2. Check the coastline reading. The reader tells pale oceans, dark silhouettes with lakes, sepia maps, painted biome maps and true heightfields apart, and says which way it read your sheet. Correct it with Paint land / Paint water, freeze an area with Protect region (shown in gold), or trace it yourself — Magnetic tracing follows the picture's own edges between the anchors you click. Auto trace offers Fine, Balanced or Smooth outlines and reports what it removed.
  3. Answer the rivers question. Under The rivers on this map, blue lines are read as rivers where they are narrow — wide water stays a lake or a sea — and the reading is drawn over your map in blue with a count. Keep the rivers I drew cuts each one into the surface as a strictly descending channel, so the hydrology solver's lowest path is your line. Let CartoVox put the rivers still takes them out of the coastline, so your continent is whole, but leaves where the water runs to the terrain, rainfall and slope the simulation solves. They are not rivers keeps the blue as water — right for straits and fjords, and the only answer that leaves an inlet cut into your land. Either way, lakes and inland seas stay water, and the finished world states in plain words which of the three it was built with.
  4. Say how big your map is. Your land on the planet takes the share you want — 4% — and CartoVox resizes the map to reach it, measuring land only and on the sphere, so ground near the poles does not count for more than it is. The panel names the full range your sheet can reach and says plainly when you ask for one it cannot. Ocean kept around your land trims the sea you painted around your own coast, which raises that ceiling; the coastline itself is never touched.
  5. Answer the questions the map leaves open — where borrowed relief should gather, how high the peaks reach, whether the land is young or worn, how deep the seas are, how wet and warm the world is. Every answer is an ordinary Advanced setting, and an unanswered question keeps the preset.
  6. Generate. Provenance records that the coastline was imported or traced and over which file.
Two things that used to catch people out
  • The map stays attached to Create. An imported or drawn map keeps replacing the geography of everything generated from that sheet until you remove it. The Foundation map row on Create names it beside the button that uses it, and Remove map is in that row and in the map review.
  • Advanced settings and the map review share a column. Use the Map review / World settings switch above them; Advanced settings opens the settings sheet, and a newly arrived map opens its review, because a coastline wants checking first.

Drawing one instead

  • A full-screen desk with an icon rail, one button per group: Navigate (pan, zoom, fit, undo), Land (land, water, outlines, islands), Relief (raise, lower, smooth, flatten, ranges), Cover (forest, grassland, desert, wetland, tundra, ice), Water (rivers), Realms (kingdom borders, cities, roads), Annotate (labels and landmarks), World (starting shape, canvas size, graticule, HD region) and Reference (trace over a picture). The ocean wraps east–west, because it is a planet.
  • Rivers are carved as descending valleys so the solver has a lowest path to follow, and the finished world reports how much of each drawn river it actually followed rather than claiming it obeyed you. Beside the river tool is the same choice the importer offers: keep the ones you draw, or let CartoVox put them.
  • Labels, borders, cities, land cover, roads and landmarks return as an author-canon layer on the finished map. Drawn borders and cities are honoured by the build — and hold their positions against the generator's next pass — and are reported when they cannot be.
  • Drop in a reference image and the coastline is previewed automatically; Mark land / Mark water and Detect coastline correct it and Apply coastline is one undoable change.
  • Save the drawing as a PNG heightmap, GeoJSON, STL or OBJ and reopen it later. Use this drawing hands it to the same coastline review an import gets.

Deep Time

Create › Deep Time is the geological foundry. Choose a Duration of 300, 420 or 600 million years, a number of Major plates (5 to 24, twelve by default), a Tectonic tempo from gentle to restless, a crust target, and whether Milankovitch cycles and the opt-in Ice, isostasy & sea level feedback model run. Look ahead keeps the run going past the era you build from, so the timeline shows where those plates go next.

Paint an initial supercontinent or let one be built, run the breakup, spreading, subduction, collision, suturing and erosion, then scrub every saved era with play, pause, four speeds and a narration of each era's dominant events. Keeping an era hands the finished world its plate inventory, welded groups, terranes, crust age and thickness, rift progress and relief — and the brief on the Create sheet still decides the climate, peoples and names the world gets on top of it. An import and a Deep Time era cannot be combined: they are competing foundations, and the app says so.

The View menu

  • Planet shows or hides the floating globe card (key P).
  • Map colors is the palette — Natural and the others. Detail is how densely the open map is redrawn from the saved fields; Auto is the default. Drawing density is not simulation detail.
  • Projection — Stretched (the simulation's own sheet), Equal Earth, Robinson, Mollweide, Web Mercator, Gott double disk. The meridian a sheet opens at is part of the view, so it resets with the zoom and the pan each time a sheet is shown.
  • Map aids — Minimap (I), Split view (V), Render (N), Time strip (H), HD region (D), Grid (G).

Asking the map why

  • Every map states what kind of thing it is — a simulated field, a deterministic derivative, a proxy, a heuristic, a render-only detail, an authored override, or a reversible scenario — with its scale, its limitations and a provenance JSON you can export.
  • Simulation health in the World drawer scores the world out of 100 and explains unusual tectonic, climate, terrain, biome, river and data-integrity signals. A finding can jump to the relevant map and cell, and some can prepare a conservative sculpt brush — but nothing changes the world until you confirm a full re-simulation, and the resulting revision records whether that repair resolved, improved, persisted or worsened its source finding.
  • The sea-level scenario explorer previews bounded coastline changes and exposed or submerged area on the sphere without changing canon or re-simulating.

Changing the world

  • The changes — Peak, Ridge, Plateau, Island, Strait, River valley, Basin, Deep water, Coast, Flatten, Smooth, Detail, Protect area, Preserve terrain, Settlement, Story pin, and Describe a region.
  • Size a brush on the map. A click places it exactly as the panel describes; holding the pointer and dragging states the footprint in kilometres on the map itself, and releasing hands the pointer the height. A stroke is a row of ordinary nudges spaced on the ground, so the same gesture means the same thing at any zoom.
  • Ridge and River valley are trails. Click each peak or bend in turn, hold any one of them to give that summit its own height, and finish on the last point. The whole crest is one edit — previewed, undone, listed and re-simulated as a single record.
  • Describe a region is the shape-with-a-sentence tool: draw a circle or a square and say what belongs there. Words naming something CartoVox can place get it placed — a named region on the map, a page in the codex, an entry in the gazetteer, carrying your own words as its lore. Words describing ground — volcanic, highlands, sunken — move the ground through the same sculpt records a hand edit would have written. Words it could not place are reported rather than dropped.
  • Select feature acts on generated things: Rename everywhere, Choose new location for a settlement, Reroute around here for a road, and band-by-band reshaping of a classification boundary.
  • Nothing changes until you apply. Terrain edits then rebuild climate, rivers, biomes, settlements, routes, names, lore and maps. Build comparison draft makes an isolated draft from the pending edits so you can see exactly what moved before keeping it — the original world is never written to. While a rebuild is running the Library shows the project as Rebuilding edits rather than as finished.

Render Studio

  • One composition per render, from 1K to 16K, with every checked ingredient also written out as its own editable PNG layer. It does not re-run the world or add simulation detail below a saved cell.
  • Art direction first, layers second. Choose one visual language, preview it, then apply it as a single undo step; then tune palette, ocean depth bands, relief depth, contour interval, grid spacing, scale-bar units and settlement density.
  • Lettering places saved realm, region, river, road and sea-lane names with a hierarchy-aware placer, and leaves as an editable labels.svg.
  • Terrain into artwork converts saved relief, tectonics, biomes, canopy, wetlands and snowline into editable vector mountains, hills, volcanoes, trees and reeds. One undo restores the procedural map; the simulation is untouched.
  • Oblique view sits beside a finished render in the saved list. It tilts the finished sheet until the terrain has a far side — a river stays in its valley and a name stays on its town, because they are the same pixels. The scale bar, compass and graticule stay flat on the paper, and the picture says on screen and inside the PNG that its vertical scale is invented. Your render is untouched; the oblique is a separate picture beside it.
  • Maps are saved with the world and included in its project backup, with their own version history.

Working a finished world

Journeys

  • Mode — On foot, Horseback, Caravan, or Sailing ship. A ship is offered only where both endpoints are reachable by sea, and says so when they are not.
  • Season — Typical year, Midwinter, Spring, Midsummer, Autumn. Objective — Balanced, Fastest, Safer, Trade road. Party size feeds supplies.
  • Land journeys report elevation, roads, supplies, dry stretches, markets and tolls. Ships instead report wind and current assistance, open-water legs, port intervals, sea hazards, and cargo and potable-water capacity.
  • Map beneath the route swaps the sheet the route is drawn on, so you can read it against biomes, rainfall, soils or plates.
  • Save journey to campaign keeps it with a campaign profile, and the journey travels into campaign exports with its weather by departure day.

Campaigns and the player view

  • A campaign profile carries an author name, a player title and introduction, a player base map, and explicit lists of visible map layers and visible lore categories.
  • Secret pins and wonders stay hidden until revealed. Discovery snapshots capture what the table knew at a moment.
  • Present player view hides the authoring interface entirely; Player atlas is the shareable reading of the same world. Multiple profiles can sit on one world.

Working a finished world

Towns, scenes and battlemaps

Settlement Studio plans a place from its street graph outward: blocks are cut from the streets, lots from the blocks, and buildings placed in the lots — rather than rectangles scattered across a footprint.

  1. Open it from World › Scenes (or Library › Scenes and encounter maps) → Create or import settlement.
  2. Choose a saved world and a place. Selecting a capital, regional centre, town or village loads its terrain, climate, hydrology, groundwater, farming and approach-road evidence automatically — you do not enter river or water information by hand. A standalone settlement with no world behind it is also possible; it starts from stated assumptions you can edit.
  3. Pick a plan. Street village along one road, houses around a common green, crossroads town, winding lanes, ring town, two joined neighbourhoods, harbour rows along a quay, hilltop terraces — or let the setting choose. Footprint bias and Building style shape the rest.
  4. Save it as a scene, then add artwork, labels, hotspots and world links in the scene editor.
  5. Export for the table. Universal VTT (.dd2vtt) — read by Foundry through the Universal Battlemap Importer, by Roll20 through the community importer, and natively by Fantasy Grounds and Arkenforge — or a Foundry scene that Foundry opens with no module at all. Choose a square of five feet, ten feet, one metre or two; the export tells you how many squares the place is before you open it somewhere it will not fit.
  • What blocks line of sight is decided, not guessed. Buildings, town walls, towers and rock block sight; fences and pens go in the objects list, because a waist-high fence stops a cart and not a view; gates become doors that open; roads and fields are not obstacles.
  • Only forges are exported as lights. A forge is a fire and the simulation says so. Nothing else in a CartoVox settlement is a light source, and the export says that the rest is the referee's to place.
  • Scenes need no world at all. Library › Scenes and encounter maps makes a canvas — square, wide, landscape, portrait or tall — with drawing layers, lights, art directions and nested scenes.
  • Height and shadow. Give a wall, tower, column or table a height in your scene's own feet or metres and it stands up, lit by one sun for the whole sheet, so a standing tower and a collapsed one stop looking the same. Twice as tall is twice as long a shadow. Nothing tilts: every footprint stays on the square it is on, and the sheet says the heights are yours rather than measured.

Names, lore, the book

Rename everything, and describe everything

These two sheets in Write › Codex are the single most-missed part of the application. Before 0.9, two fields out of twenty-six were reachable.

  • Rename everything — every named thing in the world on a single editable sheet: realms, cultures, faiths, places of power, settlements. A canonical name propagates through maps, dossiers, lore, routes, tongues, exports and later revisions.
  • Describe everything — the prose of the world in one place. Every culture's diet, burials and taboos; every ruler's character and secret; most of every faith. A typical world ships with roughly a hundred empty fields, and the sheet can show you only those.
  • Cities and towns can be described too, and what you write is read back everywhere they are read: the dossier, the vault and search.
  • Measured findings are left alone. A place of power's kind is read off the simulation, so the sheet does not offer to edit it.
  • Lore is a proposal until you accept it. Geography and measurements are derived; social history, politics and story hooks are explicitly non-canon until you accept or edit them.

The Codex

Seventeen sections: Overview, Questions this world raises, Your canon, Import lore, Lands, Gazetteer, Realms & geopolitics, Cultures, History, Faith questions, Trade & economy, Places of power, Story hooks, Naming studio, Rename everything, Describe everything, and the Physical dossier. Import lore turns CSV rows and Markdown files into author-owned vault pages with typed place, character, faction and event records, pins on the world map, and reviewed relationships. The Gazetteer is a dossier for every named thing in the world.

Chronicle

Geological Deep Time and human-scale Chronicle are deliberately separate. Chronicle can import the generated lore timeline, add authored eras and point, route or area events, retain persistent consequences, and play the changing historical map forward.

Names, lore, the book

The manuscript editor

The book lives inside the same vault as the lore, so a distance, a date or a name cannot drift between your map and your novel.

  • The binder holds books and chapters with word counts and drafts; New book, Import book, Import chapter and a trash that can be emptied. A book brings its chapters with it.
  • The formatting strip keeps nine marks — bold, italic, H2, quotation marks, bullets, Scene, [[ ]] links, undo and redo — because those are pressed as often as bold is. Everything else from the old toolbar is under More: H1, H3, paragraph styles, numbered lists, a scene break, links, alignment, indents, page break, the named styles (plain, first paragraph, verse, letter, epigraph), Note, Focus, Sprint, the shortcut sheet, the lenses and Insert map…. The Chapter menu renames, duplicates, moves and trashes the chapter you are in.
  • Lenses (under More) read what you have written: sentence rhythm, dialogue, overused words, dialogue tags, world names, passive voice. Book › Insights scores readability and passive voice per chapter and per book.
  • The panel switch — Chapter, World, Find, Style. The World panel puts the world's own names, places and dossiers beside the page, and More › Insert map… drops any sheet, plate or city plan into the text as a captioned figure.
  • Read book is a paged reading view; Outline is the structure. Export book… writes a Word document (.docx, with real footnotes), Markdown, a Scrivener package, a web page, or an EPUB.
  • It stays quick in a long book. Saving no longer re-reads every chapter in the world: typing in a large imported book went from 832 ms of blocking to 0, opening a chapter with unsaved edits from 279 ms to 38, and a single autosave from 262 ms to 8.

Questions testers keep asking

The gold button is greyed out.

A blocked control says why it is blocked rather than what it would have done — hover it, or reach it by keyboard. The usual reasons are that no brief has been written yet, or that a map is attached and still waiting for its coastline check. In the second case CartoVox offers to remove the map and generate from your settings alone; keeping it leaves you on Create with Review map one keystroke away.

I set up a new world and it rebuilt my imported map again.

An imported or drawn map stays attached to the Create sheet after the world it was brought in for is finished. The Foundation map row names it beside the button that uses it; Remove map is in that row.

My imported world came out covered in ice, or all mountains.

Heights were being scaled so that far too much of the world sat above 2,000 m; that was fixed in 0.9, and a round-trip of a saved world's own elevation now lands where it started. If it still happens, the questions in the map review — how high the peaks reach, how wet and warm the world is — are the levers, and they are ordinary Advanced settings.

The rivers on my map became sea inlets.

That was the old behaviour: the coastline reader saw blue, called it water, and a drawn river became a sea-level fjord slicing the continent. Rivers and lakes are now told apart by width, and you choose whose rivers the world gets. See importing a map.

My continent is much smaller on the planet than the number suggested.

An imported sheet is usually a continent adrift in its author's painted sea, and the old width control resized both. Use the share-of-planet control and the ocean trim together: on a test sheet that is 85% water, trimming took the same coastline from 11.5% of the planet to 34.7% without a pixel of the coast moving.

My continent is cut in half by the edge of the map.

World › Tools › Turn world, then drag sideways. 1:1 returns the world to its own centre. The tool is off until you ask for it, because a drag that quietly reprojected every marker and route made ordinary panning feel like the map was fighting back.

Where are my illustrated plates?

World › Atlas. If you deferred them or stopped the build early, Render remaining plates draws them from saved data without re-simulating anything.

How do I print a map, or the whole atlas?

Any view downloads as a PNG from World › Export. The whole illustrated set binds as one print-ready PDF — title page, contents, a page per plate at a real paper size, and a colophon that carries the seed so the world can be rebuilt from the book. Every plate is embedded at its own resolution and the export reports the dots per inch page by page rather than asserting a number.

Why is the map a rectangle?

The world is simulated on a sphere and drawn equirectangular: it wraps east–west and the poles are real poles. Plates rotate about Euler poles, winds deflect about a rotation axis, and ocean gyres need enclosed basins with a western boundary — a flat disc has none of those, so on a disc none of the geography could be derived. Use the globe card, the four-globe sheet, or an equal-area projection to check that it reads as a planet.

Something looks wrong near the pole.

A long thin landmass near a pole is usually the projection, not the terrain. Measure it on the sphere with Measure, or look at it on the globe.

How long will a build take, and how much disk?

The COST row on Create answers for your settings and learns from completed builds of the same shape on your own machine. As orientation: a Sketch with an illustrated atlas measured 30.5 seconds on a fast Apple Silicon Mac, and the example world 83.9 seconds and 179 MB. A Full build with every publication plate is the long one — about eight minutes on a high-end Mac and about thirteen on a high-end Windows machine, with the hand-drawn plates most of that. On disk: 45–100 MiB for a Quick world, 180–360 MiB for a Full one without plates, and 337–883 MiB with every publication plate. The preflight keeps a 1 GiB reserve and warns above 500 MiB.

Can I stop a build, or use the app while one runs?

Yes to both. The generation dock keeps a long build running while you work elsewhere and survives a reload. Stop after this plate finishes the current image cleanly and stores the rest; an interrupted run is detected and can be resumed from its stage checkpoint or removed.

Nothing has any descriptions. Is that a bug?

No. The generator leaves prose blank rather than inventing it, and Write › Codex › Describe everything says how many fields are open and can show you only those. They are questions left for you, not missing data.

How do I change a name everywhere it appears?

Write › Codex › Rename everything for the whole world on one sheet, or select the feature on the map and use Rename everywhere. Canonical names propagate through maps, dossiers, lore, routes, tongues, exports and later revisions.

Can I move a city, or will the generator move it back?

Move it with Sculpt › Select feature › Choose new location, or place a new one on land with at least 20% saved suitability. Cities and borders you draw hold their positions against the world's own.

I changed the terrain and everything else stayed the same.

Terrain edits are pending until Apply & re-simulate, which rebuilds climate, rivers, biomes, settlements, routes, names, lore and maps. Build comparison draft shows you exactly what moved first, without writing to the original world.

I rebuilt a Quick world at full resolution and the coastline changed.

Expected, and why the rebuild is a separate project. Broad geography stays familiar, but finer coastlines, rivers, climate boundaries, settlements, names and generated lore can all move on a finer grid. The world you chose stays exactly as it was.

Where did my world go?

Library › Trash: 30 days for a saved world, 7 for a cancelled or incomplete generation, with the recoverable size shown. Nothing is removed early to save space.

I can't find a panel I used yesterday.

Two likely causes. Settings › Workspace & layout › How much to show at once may be set to Essentials, which hides the specialist surfaces; every hidden thing says how many it is holding and gives them back in one click. Or a region is hidden in Layout — Show everything restores it.

The little explanations under the controls disappeared.

They fold away after your first few sessions, since by then they are in the way. Settings › Appearance › Explanations overrides it in either direction, and the change moves the page you are looking at rather than the next launch.

How do I move my worlds to another computer?

World › Export › Complete project backup (.cartovox) per world, restored with Library › Import project backup. To move the whole library on one machine, use Settings › Data & privacy › Change save folder — it copies existing worlds and keeps the old files as a safety copy, and never deletes the former library automatically.

Does CartoVox send anything anywhere?

No. There is no account, no telemetry and no update check; the only address it opens is the loopback dashboard it started itself. The optional local diagnostics journal is off by default and stays in your browser profile.

Can I sell what I make with it?

Yes. You may publish and sell the worlds, maps and writing you create; you may not copy, share, resell or redistribute the application itself. The licence is at Settings › Updates & support › Licence and on this site.

How do I report a bug?

Settings › Updates & support › Report a bug on Discord, or World › Tools › Report for a specific map. A useful report includes the seed, preset, tier, any Advanced settings overrides, and the exact simulation-health finding. Copy app info copies name, version, build mode and platform — never paths, keys, world names or anything you have written. Create diagnostics builds an archive of build details and redacted logs, which you can inspect before sharing.

Read before you lean on a number

What it does not do

CartoVox documents what it approximates, at length, inside the app under Help. The short version, because it saves arguments at the table:

  • Desktop is the authoring target. The mobile layout reviews a world; it does not build one.
  • Drawing density is not simulation detail. 2×–4× and Render Studio interpolate the same saved cells. Only a full-resolution rebuild adds simulated cells, and it does so in a separate project.
  • The default tectonics is one kinematic snapshot, correlated around generated ridge systems. Deep Time transports crust for hundreds of millions of years but does not solve mantle convection.
  • Four seasons, not twelve months, so Köppen boundaries are slightly coarser than a real classification. Orbital cycles are forcing, not an N-body integration. Tides are climatological, not a tide table.
  • Ocean currents are wind-driven only — there is no thermohaline conveyor. Erosion runs two passes, so valleys look young. Hydrology is regional: a directed reach graph with lake nodes and morphology classes, not fluid dynamics.
  • Soils are regional pedology, not laboratory horizons. Resources are prospects, not proven reserves — a high value means the simulated setting can form and preserve that deposit family.
  • Glacial history is reconstructed, not integrated through every ice age; small tarns and kettle ponds are reported honestly as a sub-grid density rather than drawn.
  • Land needs crust to stand on. Asking for more land than there is continental crust drains the sea until seafloor is exposed, and the run says so.
  • Atlas labels are placed, not typeset. A name that will not fit at any rung of its shrink ladder is dropped and reported rather than broken over two lines.
  • Generated lore is a proposal. Geography and measurements are derived; social history, politics and story hooks are non-canon until you accept or edit them.

Version-by-version model changes are in the release notes, each versioned in the world's own configuration, so a saved world always states which model produced it.
