CartoVox Delta V7

Let your brief win over the planet type, watch lakes settle and rivers run downhill, map states and counties, play at a native tabletop, and finish a piece in Make.

The application was called Atlas Studio until version 0.8.2. Builds are shared with invited testers on the Discord server; these notes are also readable offline inside the app.

All release notes

Delta V7 is the seventh Delta release, shared with invited testers on 12 October 2026. Your Delta access code carries over, and 500 new codes join it. The licence is unchanged since Delta V6 (version 2.3). Everything below is new or fixed since Delta V6. Installers are available for Apple Silicon Macs, Windows x64 and Linux x64, and they are smaller again because the offline starter worlds are gone. Worlds made with Delta V6 open as they are. This build lays out water, lakes and climate differently, so the same seed rebuilt here gets different ground: Library's Update to this version rebuilds a world with this build when you choose to, keeping the old copy beside it.

Highlights

  • Your brief wins over the planet type. Create composes every world as land shape, then planet type, then your brief, then Advanced. Warm, Cold, Lush, Arid and the other graded choices move the world from where the shape and planet put it, and each control's middle keeps the world's own value. Describe says where every value came from.
  • A chosen card keeps its promise. Candidates are drawn from the same settings their world is built from, and the built world is checked against the card you chose. Every land shape checks for what makes it that shape, Best brief match puts the safest matches first, and a Prefer box sits beside Must have and Avoid.
  • Lakes settle and rivers run downhill. Lakes fill, spill and merge hollow by hollow, rivers follow the actual ground, erosion follows real catchments, and moisture is carried round the globe without losing water.
  • Seasons belong to the place. Climate follows the finished terrain and is read at each place's own height; southern places have southern seasons, Mediterranean means a dry summer, and monsoon winds turn with the seasons.
  • A river through a lake is one river, named above and below the lake and ranked by its whole basin. Held passes are real cols on every road over them.
  • History and trade follow the ground. Living History conserves food; famine, migration and expansion follow places people can reach; goods cost what their journey costs; every port has a sea lane.
  • Countries, states and counties on the map. Political divisions are ready by default, with States and Counties as map layers.
  • Play inside CartoVox. A native tabletop with a GM desk, tokens, fog, live sight, doors, turn order, dice and a player window.
  • Make, a fifth workspace, with three illustrated paths to a finished piece: a playable region, maps in a chapter, or finished map artwork.
  • Find any map layer from one place, keep favourite layers, save named map views and keep shortlists of places.
  • Closing during a build asks first, and no longer freezes the app.

World measurements

  • Moisture transport no longer loses water numerically. Humidity and its named sources use local transfers weighted by spherical cell area, with conservative mixing, recycling and rainfall smoothing. Balanced transport with fixed air columns avoids artificial humidity compression; polar transport groups explicitly trade small longitudinal detail for manageable computation. Measured drift remains reported independently of ledger consistency. Rebuilding changes rainfall, lakes and ecology and costs more at Full resolution; existing saved arrays retain their values. Generator epoch 11 explains these changes before update.
  • Drawn rivers follow the delivered ground. A curved river could cut through a higher dry neighbour even when its routed cells descended. Shared river artwork now keeps safe bends and replaces only offending legs with their native course. Dry salt pans and protected ground remain subject to the height check. Routes across a pole split correctly in raster, vector and export views. Native river paths, flow and lake budgets are preserved; existing saved pictures need a redraw.
  • Moisture accounting shows numerical drift. Ledger closure is identified as bookkeeping consistency. Signed and gross transport drift are recorded by operation, with a separate review warning and explicit limits. The eddy mixing explanation is corrected; older worlds retain diagnostics from their actual solve.
  • The water-balance reading counts all water reaching the sea. World health compared land runoff with discharge at each drainage basin's single mouth and converted it with an Earth year. It missed water entering the sea beside those mouths, and it misread worlds whose year is not 365 days, so most worlds read about 70% instead of about 98%. It now sums every sea cell in the world's own year, and lake overflow is credited where it enters the sea. Settled lakes and closed basins evaporate the remaining 1-4%.
  • Lakes settle where water can stay, and rivers run downhill. Every hollow now fills until evaporation and seepage balance what flows into it, overflows into its neighbour when full, and merges with it at their shared rim. Dry land drains down the actual ground, so a river no longer climbs out of a basin, and water that cannot cover a whole map cell is no longer painted as a 1 cm lake. A desert hollow that evaporates everything it receives shows a salt flat instead. On the default-size test world this removes 190 uphill river steps and 512 phantom lakes, and that world now passes its saved-world checks, which it previously failed. The replacement seasonal water model leaves the settled lakes unchanged for a further year. Rebuilding a world may remove tiny lakes and move rivers, basins and ecology; worlds keep their previous lake checks until rebuilt.
  • Worlds with a lake joined at its brim build again. A full lake made of two hollows that meet only at ground exactly at its level could stop world generation ("standing water must be one connected surface") when another lake's overflow arrived across that join. It happened on a default-size small planet. The arriving overflow now ends at the lake's level; rivers and lakes elsewhere are unchanged.
  • Worlds with neighbouring lakes brimming at one saddle build again. When a lake overflowed into its neighbour while the neighbour was still filling, and both then filled to the same saddle, world generation could stop ("Channel routing contains a cycle"): the first lake's outflow was routed back into itself. It now crosses the saddle and leaves with its neighbour's outflow. It happened on Quick builds of the Thin air, cold desert planet; worlds that built before are unchanged.
  • Shallow standing water is drawn as wetland. Where a settled hollow's water spreads over most of a map cell without the depth to draw a lake, the cell is now marsh, swamp forest or bog for its climate instead of forest, grassland or bare alpine ground. Desert hollows remain salt flats. On the default-size test world 55 such cells change; rivers, lakes and climate do not. The cell inspector says the wetland stands on a closed hollow's floor.
  • Verified saved water observations. The explicit finite-water API can now save a separate lossless observation archive and reopen it for lake display, relief and graph export without replaying the simulation. Bounded file checks verify native water quantities, elapsed flow rates, graph paths and shoreline clipping; conflicting saves preserve existing files. Ocean surface level and numerical-source provenance are retained explicitly. This completes developer output persistence; ordinary world generation and remaining readers still need integration. Existing annual worlds remain unchanged.
  • Coherent elapsed water observations. An explicit production API can now derive a finite observation from a declared starting water state and elapsed time. Lake display, relief rendering and graph export use its same native depths, lake identities and historical channel records. Lossless public fields include time, budgets, fork links and provenance; unknown salinity and permanent lake status stay unknown. Existing annual worlds remain unchanged. Ordinary generation and the remaining readers still need adoption. Saved readers avoid source replay; interactive caching and full-size costs still need qualification.
  • Seasonal river display groundwork. The replacement water model now draws selected channels from actual elapsed flow, clips them at observed lake and ocean shores, and preserves the complete runoff and spill evidence separately. The atlas renderer accepts this display only with matching terrain, water and source context. Existing river rendering remains unchanged. Normal generation, public saved-world fields and dependent readers still need integration.
  • Native seasonal channel graph groundwork. The replacement water model exposes both downhill runoff branches and dry lake-spill tails as elapsed interval volumes. Reach paths preserve forks and joins, with flow values for individual edges and mean rates based on actual season durations. Hydraulic lake connections remain separate from dry rivers. Visible river selection now has a guarded renderer path; production readers still need integration, and hillslope flow is retained evidence.
  • Observed seasonal lake catalogue groundwork. The replacement water model now records actual lake cells, levels, areas and volumes at elapsed seasonal endpoints. Terrain identities follow parent joins and child splits; dry basins stay dry, and incomplete years retain missing seasons. These observations prepare shared readers without assigning salinity or permanent-lake labels.
  • Elapsed seasonal water recovery groundwork. Exact finite-water saves can recover the river, shoreline and spill records for elapsed substeps in the current year, or the preceding year at a complete boundary. Recovery checks the exact saved water state and budgets before returning results; partial years retain their missing seasons. This prepares the replacement model's readers and does not yet replace production lakes or public river maps.
  • Self-contained finite-water progress groundwork. The replacement water solver saves exact terrain, cell metrics and prepared water inputs alongside pool depths, elapsed seasons and cumulative budgets. It can restore the next step with the same water and future flow records without the original inputs or rounded display fields. Repeated identical forcing steps share storage; archive and metadata size checks prevent unreadable progress saves. Changed terrain, forcing or solver context rejects incompatible progress; failed or stale saves preserve the previous snapshot. Dry runoff is measured directly so rounded wet fractions cannot invent or erase water. These standalone checkpoints prepare production integration; generated lakes, the public river catalogue and their consumers still await the complete model switch.
  • Finite river-graph integration groundwork. The replacement water solver now records distributed runoff stopped at moving lake shores and dry spill tails stopped at receiving water, with cell budgets and actual spill timing. Native annual transient checks preserve the solver's water state exactly. This prepares the replacement graph; production lake/river integration and public saved-world integration remain in development.
  • Finite lake-storage integration groundwork. The replacement storage solver now accepts actual production rain-and-snowmelt depths, native season durations and runoff exclusions, with immutable source snapshots and budget checks. Frozen precipitation cannot sustain a liquid pool in the analytical reference. Production lakes and the public river graph still await integration; bounded transient runs are not labelled permanent or equilibrated worlds.
  • Lakes use rain and modeled snowmelt. Direct lake input now follows the shared rain/snow partition and sums water over the actual lake footprint. Frozen precipitation retained as snow no longer creates direct liquid outflow. New saves and GIS bundles retain annual/seasonal source depths; checks compare lake budgets with those native inputs. Rebuilding may change lake levels and downstream seasonal flow, and generator notices explain that change. Existing worlds stay unchanged. The annual lake-area approximation still needs finite storage and time integration; these source corrections do not complete it.
  • Aquifer recharge uses its actual water source. Aquifer reports now read infiltration-derived shallow recharge from the groundwater solver, replacing the unrelated quarter-of-runoff estimate. New saves and GIS bundles retain annual recharge and baseflow, with checks that reports, renewal classifications and graph provenance agree. Maps and settlement readings use the corrected reports. Baseflow is already included in runoff; renewal remains a model interpretation rather than measured fossil age or a pumping allowance. Existing saved worlds are preserved, and inconsistent exports leave previous downloads intact.
  • Height-datum migration history stays with the world. Saved checks now verify recorded author-height translations and the world's sea-zero settings. Scientific/GIS bundles include the numeric history while removing private edit IDs and author-edit fingerprints. Completion records detect a changed, missing or added report, and revision checkpoints keep its exact local copy. Later edits do not falsely invalidate historical evidence. Older completion records that omitted an existing migration report need a newly verified build; existing reports and worlds are preserved.
  • Erosion follows real catchments. Incision and floodplain build-up now use contributing area routed over actual ground, stopping at pits rather than escaping across an imaginary filled surface. The Erosion Lab uses the same catchment and ground-slope calculations. Rebuilding can change valleys and floodplains; this remains a terrain-shaping approximation, without a sediment budget or timed lake spill simulation.
  • Ground processes follow real slopes. Erosion, infiltration, wetlands, geology and settlement placement now use actual terrain descent. A filled routing surface no longer makes sloping ground inside a basin seem flat. New saves retain that measurement for independent checks, GIS export and the cell inspector, where underwater readings are labelled bed slope. Rebuilding can change local terrain, water and ecology; saved-stage reuse carries the new fields through story and realm edits. River and lake physics remain under development.
  • Unfinished drainage repairs retain their basins. A proposed fill or cut no longer counts as completed terrain work. Remaining depressions keep their water budgets, and rebuilding recalculates affected lakes, rivers, erosion, ecology and settlements. Saved checks compare basin footprints and routing potential with actual ground; older worlds remain readable. Compact test worlds lose their dry uphill river edges, while full-resolution river routing and time-resolved lake physics remain under development.
  • Drainage tries another bounded escape. If the first route cannot be cut within the existing depth, length or protection limits, repair searches for a nearby descending route. Accepted corridors remain open, and new saves record the chosen method for independent checks. Original protection and ocean heights stay intact. Rebuilding may change local ground and climate. This reduces contradictions in the full saved-terrain routing trial, but some cuts expose further unresolved basin routing; river and lake physics remain under development and strict scientific checks continue to fail them.
  • Automatic drainage cuts keep descending. Cuts now follow lower pockets along an escape instead of returning to the starting pit's higher level. Later fills cannot close the accepted path. New saves retain each path for independent height, neighbour and length checks; version-1 saves remain readable. The inspector shows terrain changes for both versions. Rebuilding can change local ground and its reconciled climate, so the generator notice records that change. Rejected cuts leave unresolved basins; this does not remove the remaining dry uphill rivers or qualify the lake model.
  • River contradictions are easier to inspect. Health and saved-world validation now check every recorded dry river edge, so a local climb cannot hide behind a reach's overall descent or a depression label. Findings show the climb and link to ground measurements at its location. Older health reports gain the check without rewriting their recorded findings. Intact worlds remain saveable with explicit physical errors in their build receipt; scientific validation and release checks still fail those contradictions. This exposes remaining uphill rivers; it does not claim to repair them.
  • Pressure readings use consistent units. Weather Year and Climate Lab now call the signed seasonal values "Pressure anomaly" in hPa. Map titles and saved-field descriptions agree; these values are departures, not absolute surface pressure. Existing climate arrays and Weather Year drawings stay unchanged, and old reader revisions refresh when reopened.
  • UV maps follow seasonal snow and rainfall. New worlds save a seasonal snow-reflection proxy, so snow that melts before year-end still affects the relevant seasons, and year-end storage no longer represents every season. Unequal season lengths now preserve the annual rainfall used for cloud; bounded sampling avoids erasing valid rain patterns when enlarging compressed climate fields. Map notes and export provenance show the reading's actual source fields. Older worlds explicitly retain the end-storage snow proxy until rebuilt; their UV drawings still receive the rainfall-reading fix.
  • Snow no longer loses water at an arbitrary storage limit. Seasonal melt uses a defined starting snow store instead of ten warm-up years and a 20,000 mm cap. When snowfall exceeds melting, the surplus remains in storage and is shown separately as annual accumulation. Inspectors and place reports distinguish end-of-year storage from winter snow or glacier age. Existing worlds keep their saved readings until explicitly rebuilt.
  • Scientific maps keep their evidence when enlarged. Higher drawing density no longer creates negative fertility/productivity or values above native maxima. Missing readings stay missing, and coastal readings do not mix with values outside their land/ocean domain. Scientific coastlines, water masks, resource winners and threshold classes keep their native footprint.
  • Consistent scenarios and reading keys. Sea-level scenarios and seasonal temperature ranges use native observations before enlargement. Automatic colour scales use the same native bounds as their legends. Unknown rainfall, resource prospects and aquifer measurements no longer produce misleading colours. Relief artwork retains its separate smoothing rules; higher drawing density does not add simulation detail. Cached drawings refresh automatically.
  • Marine boundaries respect the globe. Marine temperature mixing, coast proximity and fisheries plumes no longer connect the north edge to the south edge. Nutrient plumes also stop at intervening land. Existing saved numerical worlds can redraw these derived maps; cached drawings refresh automatically.
  • Honest ocean-current readings. Climate Lab, place dossiers, cell inspection and scientific exports identify relative current strength rather than claiming a calibrated speed in metres per second. Export and API metadata explain the normalization. Circulation values and wind speed units are unchanged.
  • Ordinary towns join the road network. Generated roads now include local town tracks while retaining links between major cities. Reachable groups connect within the configured endpoint-distance limit. Ground barriers and authored removals are respected. Travel, route exports and history use the same saved paths. Existing worlds gain the network when rebuilt.
  • Sea lanes consider reachable ports first. Nearby ports on a separate waterway, or connections removed by the author, no longer hide a farther valid destination. Land and lake barriers, isolated zones and the configured distance limit remain effective. Existing worlds gain this correction when rebuilt; it does not imply measured shipping traffic or capacity.
  • Accurate town-access diagnostics. Connectivity reports now count named towns without recorded links instead of displaying an uncomputed zero. This reports the actual network; it does not create missing roads or imply that cross-country travel is impossible.
  • Settlement shores agree with the saved terrain. Low coastal ground keeps its actual fractional height when measuring the shoreline, matching Render Studio's zero-elevation contour in the checked cases. High-latitude shore checks now use the actual neighboring grid cells; water several columns away no longer counts as touching the town. Recorded ports retain their separate harbor treatment. New plan metadata records the corrected reading version. Existing saved plans keep their geometry, and numerical worlds need no rebuild for a fresh plan.
  • Climate follows the finished terrain. After glacial and river erosion, generation recalculates climate on the final heights and routes the resulting water without eroding the land a second time. Biomes, resources and settlements then read that reconciled state. Painted climates and supplied ground remain effective. This adds calculation time, and rebuilding can change generated settlements and borders as they respond to the corrected climate.
  • Recorded terrain/climate agreement. New numerical saves include evidence linking final terrain, temperature and rainfall. Publication checks it against the archive and summary; scientific exports include the report. Older saves remain readable without inventing evidence for their earlier calculations.
  • Lakes conserve their routed water. Every preserved basin, including small lakes, participates in flow accounting. Inflow counts all tributaries; outflow follows both downstream branches without replacing other water at the outlet. Interleaved basins are intercepted before their budgets are solved. New saves carry independently checked lake budgets and discharge evidence; older saves remain readable and need re-simulation for the corrected results.
  • Drainage repairs can change the visible ground. Small eligible hollows are filled and shallow obstructions are cut within declared limits before the final climate and water solve. Supplied, kept and built ground is protected. New saves retain before/after evidence, and the cell inspector shows local changes and protection. Remaining uphill channel segments are reported explicitly; the bounded repair does not resolve every depression.
  • Terrain evidence survives cached rebuilds. Exact height differences keep their archive precision through checkpoints. Publication checks their saved surfaces, protection, bounds and volumes, and rejects missing or unsupported repair evidence. Older worlds remain readable without invented proof.
  • Lake maps and descriptions share the same meaning. Small freshwater lakes are no longer labelled salt pans or used as dust sources. Size alone no longer implies seasonal drying. Lake dossiers use the saved lake identity instead of borrowing facts from another lake of similar area, and distinguish a recorded outlet from a proven route to the sea. Inspectors label the model's limits.
  • Safer cached rebuilds. Saved ground retains the inputs needed by water and population accounting. If a late cache miss requires full generation, the rebuild continues its existing publication rather than failing at a second commit point.
  • Regional measurements respect the sphere. Continent temperatures, rainfall and heights, desert measurements, forest canopy and realm biome shares use the ground area each map cell represents. Realm lake areas use their actual locations; older saves without that measurement say the area was not recorded. The Worldbook, realm handbooks and Compendium share these saved measurements.
  • Lore geography uses the world's size and borders. Feature distances, story hooks and author questions use the planet's configured radius. Opposite polar realms no longer become neighbours through map wrapping, and narrow territories remain visible when determining shared borders.
  • Stronger numerical save checks. New worlds declare a complete archive format. Before replacing a numerical save, generation checks required fields, dimensions, data types, category IDs and finite values, including seasonal frames. The saved-world validator distinguishes missing modern provenance from older formats and reports corrupt data without crashing.
  • Honest measurement labels. The cell inspector labels melt input divided by runoff as a ratio, which may exceed one after water losses. Scientific exports explain that ratio, boundary intensity's actual range and the direction of the aridity index.
  • Interrupted builds cannot pass as finished worlds. Generation validates the saved world and records fingerprints of its completed maps, data and generated documents. After a force-close, startup verifies that record instead of relying on an older revision. Partly written worlds stay marked incomplete; a completed build that missed its last revision can be recovered safely.
  • Rebuild unfinished worlds from the Library. A compact recovery action uses recorded settings and applied edits while preserving writing and notes. Missing source maps or invalid settings are explained before rebuilding. Cancelling an incomplete world's recovery no longer incorrectly marks it ready.
  • Configuration migrations report rejected values. Settings loading and its migration report share the same conversion rules, so invalid values cannot silently fall back to defaults while being reported as unchanged.
  • Disabled exports cannot leave an older world live. Rebuilding without the optional numerical archive or JSON summary moves an earlier copy into recoverable safety storage. The completed-build check rejects leftover stale files. Author notes remain untouched, and safety copies stay in backups.
  • Mediterranean means a dry summer. Climate classes apply the full Köppen–Geiger summer-dry test: the driest summer month is under 40 mm, under a third of the wettest winter month and drier than every winter month, and the summer season as a whole is drier than winter. One dry shoulder month used to be enough, so monsoon coasts with rainy summers read "Mediterranean" (half of the default test world's Cs/Ds area had wetter summers than winters). Settlements classed as Mediterranean there fell from 87 to 8. Winter-dry climates use the same paired test.
  • Season temperatures belong to the place. Seasonal frames are saved at half resolution, so a valley town shared its season reading with the mountain beside it, and season tables could sit degrees outside the town's own coldest and warmest months. Dossier season tables, settlement context and the travel planner now move each reading from the frame's average height to the place's own with the world's lapse rate. On the default test world 77 settlements had a season colder than their coldest month or warmer than their warmest; none do now.

World evidence and lore

  • Rivers and lakes are counted as the water itself runs. Two rivers that reach the sea at the same spot are now two rivers; one used to vanish into the other, keeping only the larger basin. Branches that end in a lake which overflows to the sea now count as reaching the sea (the default test world read 77% of branches reaching the ocean; it is about 98%), and every lake is counted as open or closed. A lake whose water touches only corner to corner is one lake with one name, not two. The Worldbook's lake count and table follow the same lakes, and its salt-pan figure says how much is lakes that dry to salt and how much is dry desert floors. River reach exports add the discharge at each reach's end and say that their mean is the largest along the reach. The Worldbook's river table and each river's dossier say whether it ends at the sea, in a named lake (which may overflow on as another river) or in a closed basin, instead of giving a lake shore as its mouth.
  • A river that runs through a lake is one river. The river feeding a lake and the river leaving it now share one name and number, measured from the farthest source across its lakes. On the default test world the largest river reads 3,771 km instead of 1,023 km, the river that read "174 km" is now its upper course, and 2 of the twenty largest rivers end in a lake instead of 7. Rivers above those lakes give up their own names (19 of 60); every other name stays.
  • River pages say where a river rises and which lakes it runs through. The Worldbook table gains a "runs through" column; a river page gives its source, its lakes (linked when named) and, for a river ending in a lake, the river it drains on as. The inspector and Why here? name the river a channel belongs to above a lake as below it, exported river reaches carry that name, and town rivers and map lettering follow the river's own course.
  • Your river names survive the join. Rebuilding keeps every river's number and name; a name you gave a river that is now another river's upper course is held under Released in the rebuild review, never silently dropped.
  • A river through a lake is ranked by its whole basin. A river whose lake spills straight to its end was ranked by the stretch above the lake; it now ranks, and is numbered, by the whole river. The help for year and day length now lists the water stage among their readers, since each river's mean flow at its end is worked out from the world's own year.

Realm prose no longer calls unmapped wells strategic, and the Worldbook measures each climb from the road's lower approach.
  • Held passes say whether they can be gone round. Each pass is closed, with the ground across its road for 120 km either side, and the cheapest overland route between the road's approaches is found on the same terrain the roads follow. A pass is the only practical crossing when no way round costs less than twice as much, easily bypassed when one costs at most a quarter more, and otherwise has a dearer alternative. On the default test world 1 of 18 passes is the only practical crossing, 6 have a dearer alternative (32–95% more) and 11 are easily bypassed (5–24% more). The Worldbook's pass table gains an "if it is closed" column; generation takes about 0.1 s longer.
