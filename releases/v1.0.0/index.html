CartoVox 1.0

Free for everyone: your own species and biomes, flat worlds, regions and night skies, plates you can edit, and a tabletop your players join.

The application was called Atlas Studio until version 0.8.2. CartoVox 1.0 and later are free downloads; Delta builds before it were shared with invited testers on the Discord server. These notes are also readable offline inside the app, under Settings › Release notes. How to use the app is in the guide.

Highlights

CartoVox 1.0, dated 22 November 2026, follows Delta V16.1 and is the first public release. It is free, needs no invite, access code, account or check-in, and works offline. Installers are for Apple Silicon Macs (macOS 14 or later), Windows 10/11 x64 and Linux x64 (glibc 2.34 or newer); download them here. At first launch you read and accept licence 3.1 and sign it with your name beside the date; the signature stays on your computer, and Settings › Licence shows who signed and when. If you used a Delta build, the first time 1.0 opens it shows a card for each headline feature, each with Show me; Help and the command palette open it again. The full account is in the release notes.

  • Nothing about you is sent: CartoVox only checks for new versions, and you can turn that off. The Delta usage report and its access codes are gone, and your worlds stay on your computer. Help › Check for updates reads the update notes; Settings › Updates & support changes when it checks. On Mac and Windows it can update and restart once your work is saved, checking each download against our own signature first; on Linux it downloads an installer for you to run. Privacy.
  • Your own species and ecosystems: create and duplicate species, describe their habitat and relationships, and set the ecosystem slider (Very realistic, Balanced or Very flexible) to see how food, hosts and other partners shape their ranges. Find a species in search or the Compendium, follow links to its prey or host, and open its range on the map. More built-in wildlife lives in mountains, deserts, wetlands and the deep sea.
  • Biomes of your own: add up to twelve in Create › Advanced › Rules of the world › Your biomes, or from the Biomes map or the species editor. A biome can follow a climate, its signature species, or both, and shows on every biome map, key, plate and export. Eight more rules set where the tundra, forest, desert and Mediterranean lines fall, and a set of rules travels as a short code. The Climate Lab rebuilds a world under changed rules. Create.
  • Flat worlds and disc worlds: a flat world (experimental) is a rectangle or an ellipse with open sea, a wall or a wrap past its edge, and its own sun. It gets climate, soils, natural regions, realms, campaigns, Render Studio and exports like any world. A disc world gives you more say over where its warm and cold rings fall.
  • Regions of your own: World › Tools › Regions draws a region with a lasso, a polygon or a brush, names it and says what kind it is. It may cross coasts, realms and natural regions, gets its own gazetteer page and atlas plate, and works with Find places, [[links]] and the GeoJSON and GeoPackage exports.
  • Shape the night sky: in Sky & planet, Edit constellations opens a star chart where you move, brighten, add and delete stars and draw your own constellations. Each can carry its story and a name in every people’s tongue; the Compendium gains a Night sky page, and a star-chart handout prints as PDF or PNG. Sky & planet also follows moon orbits and their tides, and the star chart shows the year’s meteor streams.
  • Atlas plates you can edit: Edit plate drags, resizes, hides or swaps any stamp, adds new ones, frames the plate and sets its title and layers; Edit labels moves the names. Five new plate looks join CartoVox and Parchment: Woodcut print, Watercolour wash, Medieval portolan, Admiralty chart and Night atlas, each drawn in its own tradition. Atlas.
  • Render Studio: the same five looks arrive as art directions, each a whole look in one click. Place camera stops to preview a terrain flyover and export it as animated WebP, PNG frames or MP4, and tilt a finished continent, region or disc sheet under the World map’s skies. Render Studio.
  • Players at the table: players who join from their own phone, tablet or computer now take part: move their own token, pin notes, roll dice and propose a journey, as you allow for everyone or one player at a time. Nothing moves under the fog, your moves at the desk win, and the session recap records what players did. To play over the internet, use Tailscale or another VPN. Tabletop.
  • Books and shared worlds: download a World Bible as PDF or EPUB with atlas maps, place pages, history, peoples and tongues, with a player edition that keeps to what the campaign has revealed. Share a world as a web map with zoomable layers, a searchable Compendium and a timeline that opens offline from index.html. Books export as store-ready EPUB and Kindle e-books. Export.
  • Explore the map by keyboard: Tab into the World map for Keyboard map; its cursor reads the terrain, biome, climate and elevation, and jumps between named places, realms and rivers. Screen readers hear names and tooltips in your interface language as soon as you change it.
  • A new look, and a calmer start: the Atlas theme (Atlas Dark and parchment Atlas Day) follows your computer’s light or dark setting, and the Delta themes stay in Settings › Appearance. The welcome is now one card on the title screen, where Example world opens Eofordal. Ask or do anything at the top of each workspace finds maps, places, tools and exports, and every town and village shows its streets when you zoom in.
  • Measured again: on the reference M5 Pro Mac, from source, a Sketch world took 14.87 seconds, Quick 27.45 seconds and Full 81.55 seconds (medians of three runs on 22 November 2026), so Create quotes about 15 seconds, 30 seconds and 80 seconds; a Full world with every publication plate took about 14 minutes. These are not timings of the packaged installers. Build.

Full notes

CartoVox 1.0 follows Delta V16.1, the Windows update to Delta V16. Invited testers received Delta V16 on 13 November 2026.

Night sky and impacts

  • Sky & planet follows moon orbits for a reported interval and compares the estimated tides and reflected light of moons around a giant host.
  • The star chart shows three annual meteor-stream encounters on worlds with an atmosphere. The crater atlas estimates surviving relief and highlights exposed ejecta where the map can resolve it.

  • Flat world (experimental), a first look at worlds that are a flat map with no planet behind them. Its door is in Create beside the blank canvas (shown when Essentials is off). Choose a rectangle or an ellipse and its size, what lies past the edge (open sea, a wall at the world's end, or a world that wraps round east to west and north to south), and its sun: one that circles above with seasons, day and night, one that stands still with endless day beneath it and night past its reach, or two suns. The world gets its own plates and mountains, sea, sunlight, temperature, winds blowing from cold to warm (turned as far as you ask), rain carried in from the sea, rivers, lakes and biomes. A flat world is saved in your Library like any world and opens on the World map as a flat sheet with a scale in km: its card stands where the Planet card does, the pointer reads km east and north of the middle, Measure draws straight lines in km, clicking the map reads the ground, climate and water there, and the map on screen, its heightmap and its features as GeoJSON in km can be downloaded. Sculpt on its card raises or lowers the ground or paints land or sea, then rebuilds the whole world with your marks. A flat world's climate runs month by month from its own sun: the sea warms and cools more slowly than the land and lags behind it, snow and sea ice brighten the cold, winds blow from cold highs to warm lows and swing round them as far as the world spins, ranges turn the wind and cast rain shadows, coasts draw sea breezes, and each sea turns in its own current, crowding against its western shore when the world spins and carrying warmth with it and passing round its islands. How much the spin strengthens toward the top of the sheet is yours to set in the sheet (Spin gradient). Lakes now stand only in hollows deep and wide enough to outlast the mud their rivers bring, filling as far as their water allows, so a dry basin holds a shrunken salt lake; the rest are plains the rivers cross. Rivers keep a year of their own from the months' rain and snowmelt: perennial, seasonal or dry outside the wet months, shown on a new River regimes map and read when you click a river. Its new Ocean currents and Köppen climates maps sit beside the others; the climate chart, the almanac (sunrise and sunset as the flat sun comes within reach, and the second sun under twin suns), the Climate Lab's seasons, checks, comparisons and Apply (a few seconds' rebuild), the Climate panel's rainfall and temperature, and the Weather year open on a flat world, and Day & night on its card shows where it is night at any month and hour. A flat world now also gets its soils, rock, mineral resources, forest cover, wild crops and groundwater, worked out the way a planet's are and measured straight across the sheet. And it now has its peoples: capitals, regional centres and towns, realms with borders, roads and sea lanes, all named in the world's tongues (your own included), with histories, faiths, provinces, arms and a Compendium. Town and realm names stand on the World map, a new Realms and roads map shows them, clicking the map names the realm and the nearest town, Find places lists every named place, the gazetteer's pages say where a place lies in km from the middle of the sheet, the Naming studio renames anything, and the GeoJSON download carries the realms, roads and towns in km. A Sculpt rebuild keeps every town, realm and name its edit did not reach, and the names you chose. A flat world now has natural regions too, named like a planet's and shown in the Regions panel, the gazetteer's lands and on the map, and you can draw your own regions over them; its wild life is parted into realms by sea, mountain and desert, and its crops spread within them. The realm roster, the Nations panel, roads and walls you draw, towns you move, add or remove, and the Realms & settlements choices all work on a flat world and rebuild it in a few seconds. The Journey Lab plans routes, itineraries, reach maps, news and pointcrawls across a flat world in straight km, provinces, markets, trade flows and price lists are measured across the sheet, and a town opens in Settlement Studio and the city builder as a planet's does. A people is called cool-country by how cold its homeland is, not by how far up the sheet it lies. Living History runs on a flat world, its wars and trade measured across the sheet, and Render Studio draws a flat world in its own shape with a km grid and a scale bar that holds everywhere. A flat world has an atlas too: its plates (the whole sheet, its continents, a plate for each realm and for your own regions marked for the atlas) are framed on the sheet in km and drawn flat, with relief, forests, rivers, coasts, realms, roads, towns, names and the key, a grid of round km instead of latitude and longitude, a km scale bar and north at the sheet's top. The label and plate editors work on them (the plate editor frames a plate in km), closer looks zoom into the world's own ground, the plates bind into the atlas PDF, and a rebuild marks the plates it changed for redrawing. A flat world now has continent and region maps too: open a continent from Continents & regions or draw a rectangle on the sheet, and it is simulated again two, four or eight times finer, nested in the world as on a planet, with no projection (the map is a window of the sheet, in km), the edge you chose respected and the seams of a world that wraps joined. They carry the world's rivers down, offer the same map types, read their places and Find places, take your own regions, export their heightmaps and engine packs, sharpen as you zoom, and zooming far into the World map carries on into a built continent's map.   • Campaign template cards on a flat world, a region or a disc now show that world's own map round the town each campaign starts in, with its start and destination marked, instead of an empty band; and the Scene maps page's Settlement and Bring your map cards are drawn pictures of a walled river town and of a hand-drawn cave fitted to the scene grid, matching the canvases above them in light and dark themes.
  • The tabletop's tools are four groups of icons (move and measure, reveal and cover, mark the table, map view), each naming itself and saying what it does when you point at it, with the chosen tool's settings after them, in place of two rows of text buttons.

Imported and drawn worlds

  • The drawing canvas has a regular latitude and longitude grid. Coastline review keeps the drawing’s proportions, placement shows its land shares on the picture and globe, and Build asks for the name once.
  • A world built from a drawing or imported picture follows the coastline you confirmed. Shallow water beside bright land stays water, and low islands beside deep water keep their width when the picture changes size.
  • On an ink or parchment map, the names lettered beside a forest no longer widen it: letters of a name are no longer read as tree marks. Names whose letters run into each other, or two names written over one another, are no longer read as an inked river.
  • The Draw canvas has Overwrite, as Sculpt does. Tick it for Land, Water, Islands or Outline and the new ground replaces what is under it instead of building on it: Land brings mountains down to lowland, Water fills deep sea up to open ocean, and new islands or a filled outline stand alone, the old coast round them going under the sea. Each tool keeps its own answer.
  • Outline has Exact shape: the coast is the line you traced, corner to corner, with no soft shore or wobble along it. Both choices are kept with the drawing. Both Fill land and Cut water with Exact shape keep the shore in the world you build from the drawing, too: the world map follows the line to the cell, and the continent, region and disc maps and the finer views draw it exactly, with no generated bays or spits along it. Each one is listed in Sculpt's Edits as an exact coast from your drawing, and removing it there gives that coast back to the simulation on the next rebuild. Reapplying an edited drawing updates its coast records. Undo restores the earlier outline. Repainting through a traced shore releases that outline so the built world follows the new paint.

The living world

  • Create and duplicate species, describe their habitat and relationships, and use the ecosystem slider to see how food, hosts and other partners shape their ranges. The map also includes more built-in wildlife for mountains, deserts, wetlands and the deep sea.
  • Make a biome from the Biomes map or the species editor. Give it a climate, choose signature species that can share the same ground, or use both rules together.
  • Find a species in search or the Compendium, follow links to its prey or host, and open its range on the map. Compare your own species on continent and region maps, inspect their suitability at a place, and carry their rules in shared biomes and recipes.
  • Weather Year explains how it estimates heatwaves, gusts and the polar vortex from the world's saved seasons.
  • The winds that carry moisture turn a little less across pressure lines. This helps rain reach deep tropical lowlands, especially on supercontinents.
  • Weather year shows where drifting sea ice may compress and how ocean nutrients rise and fall through the seasons.
  • Calendar, Weather Year, and the observatory use the same saved climate orbit for the equinoxes, solstices and closest approach. Builds at a negative precession year now move the orbit in the correct direction.
  • Air crossing a mountain range keeps its rain shadow farther into the valleys behind it, including where it rises over a lower ridge.
  • Rivers run around the towns and paved ground you describe. Rain on the streets drains at the bank, and a natural lake keeps its shore where it reaches the town. Sharpen and finer maps follow the same footprint.
  • On a disc world the sun's year follows the disc's own rings, not the globe it is simulated on: the almanac's sunrise, sunset and daylight, a place's seasons, the Satellite night side and the light under a sun over the hub all use the climate each ring has, and a place without a name is given by its distance from the hub. With Day & night open over a disc's map, the map shows the night side and the town lights at the moment you pick.
  • Subtropical return currents begin turning offshore before their simulated poleward transport ends. Climate measurements found a large slowdown and a warm-layer regression in the new heat transport, so heat again follows the established current advection and restoring calculation.
  • Ocean gyres close against the shape of the coast, including its connected fringe, and smooth their interior over water before forming the boundary current. This replaces the separate boundary layer on each latitude.
  • On finer maps, carried rivers take their valley floors from the parent channel beds. A course that crosses a tile keeps its floor even when its ends are outside it, and larger rivers cut deeper within the available fall. A river that leaves and returns to a detailed area keeps the same valley floor on both sides of its edge. Tributaries aim into the main river at about forty degrees while the main stem keeps its smooth continuation; terrain constraints still take priority over the joining angle.
  • The cold fronts trailing behind the winter storms now reach further toward the tropics, so the seas and coasts at 25–35° get a little more rain (about 6% more at 25–30°, close to Earth's), and the wettest belts of the westerlies at 40–50° a little less. The desert interiors stay as dry as before.
  • The sea's seasons now follow Earth's: through the year its temperature moves a couple of degrees in the tropics and about six in the storm belts, instead of two to three times that, so winter seas are milder and summer seas cooler (a sea at 45° now runs from about 10 °C to 17 °C, where it ran from 4 °C to 22 °C). The seas and coasts at 10–20° get a little more rain, closer to Earth's, and the westerlies still blow hardest in winter.
  • Winter storms now form over the warm currents off the east coasts and run out across the sea, as they do over the Kuroshio and the Gulf Stream: the seas at 25–40° beside a warm current get much more winter rain, the subtropical seas as a whole now get about Earth's rain (some 15% more than before), and the wettest belts of the westerlies a little less. The east coasts themselves stay about as wet as before, and the deserts as dry.
  • Every wide ocean now has an overturning current, as the Atlantic does: warm tropical water runs up its western side and colder water comes back down its eastern side. The warm currents off the east coasts are a little warmer and faster, and the seas off the west coasts of the continents in the subtropics are about a degree colder, closer to the cold seas off California, Morocco, Chile and Namibia. Rain changes little; the subtropical lands are slightly drier.
  • The storm belt now runs poleward across each ocean as it crosses it, as it does from Japan to Alaska and from Florida to Norway: the subtropical seas off the west coasts of the continents get about half the rain they did, close to the dry seas off California and Morocco, while the seas off the east coasts get more. The west coasts at 30–35° are a little drier and those at 40–45° a little wetter; the deserts stay as they were.
  • A town where roads meet, or where a road crosses its river, now grows for it when the world is built, by up to 30%, instead of only in the city builder. The World map, the place's page, the gazetteer, the Compendium, exports and the city builder all give the same figure, and the place's page says how much the roads added. Editing one part of the world leaves the towns elsewhere at the size they were, and a region map's own towns and villages grow by the region's roads the same way.

Writing, lore and history

  • Manuscript link suggestions put names that start with what you type first and let you link to the world by its name.

The player edition uses the campaign's public maps, revealed places, published news and calendar. Private festivals stay in the author's copy.
  • Step through the Chronicle map one era or year at a time. Export saved history as an animated WebP or GIF with dated borders, towns, routes and events. The map labels inferred borders.
  • The Manuscript journey calendar compares scene dates with seasonal travel times, flags the first impossible arrival and lets you mark intentional exceptions.
  • The web of relations keeps the name you are typing for a new tie when the Compendium refreshes.
  • The Chronicle keeps the relief map in view while a history run prepares its new borders, so the present-day realm map does not flash in between.
  • Adding the first dated era or event unlocks the Chronicle’s year selector for keyboard and screen reader users.
  • Undo or redo a world rename while its atlas labels refresh. The progress dock follows the redraw, and the open atlas shows the new lettering when it finishes.
  • Opening Flags goes straight to the flag editor. The Lore editor loads the Arms preview only when you open Arms.
  • The Lore editor confirms saved changes while the map list is still loading.
  • Opening a dossier in the Manuscript World panel brings its title into view below the toolbar.
  • Compendium dossiers and open atlas plates stay available when you rename a world or undo the rename in the Lore editor.
  • Project review opens on changes that need your judgment. Story prompts, similarities between generated names and optional atlas sheets are under Possibilities. Each question still opens where you can answer it.
  • Chronicle opens a saved era on its chosen map. A world with an empty Chronicle still shows its own ground behind the first page. Other workspaces can open without waiting for the Chronicle's map to draw.
  • Read coats of arms in your interface language in the Arms editor, dossiers and Compendium captions.
  • Give a town its own faith in Describe, or leave Faith blank to follow its realm's written faith. The town's dossier, Compendium page and search all use it, and new characters and Quick NPCs take their home's faith unless you give them another. Faith can also be edited in the town's vault page.
  • Checks on a long book whose scenes are dated and travel between towns finish many times sooner: a 150-scene book with several hundred journeys took about 25 seconds and now takes about a second. Running Checks again after an edit no longer measures every journey afresh while the world itself has not changed. The findings are exactly the same.
  • Books export as store-ready e-books. Export › E-book (.epub) makes an EPUB that every reading app opens, with a cover drawn from your map or plate (or your own uploaded cover art), title and copyright pages, a contents list with Parts holding their chapters, chapter-opening pictures, your book maps with descriptions for readers who cannot see them, and the series, ISBN, description and subjects a store lists the book under. Kindle e-book (.epub) makes one that follows Amazon KDP's rules. Each is checked before it saves, and any problems are listed so you can fix them or download anyway. Both are in Export › Presets as Ebook (EPUB) and Kindle. Comments and unaccepted suggestions are never included; footnotes become pop-up notes unless you choose to leave them out.
  • A new world's Chronicle opens on a history drafted from its own lore instead of an empty box: the Compendium's History, plus the founding of each leading realm at its capital, the borders between realms, the faith questions and the places of power, each placed on the map as a proposal you can accept, change or delete. Nothing is saved until you choose Keep this history (editing any entry keeps it too), or Start from a blank page to set it aside. A Chronicle you have already written in is never drafted over, and Undo takes either choice back.
  • The Chronicle reads like the Compendium: one flat sheet with the history in the reading face, a first page that shows the world's own map behind three plain ways to begin (from the world's history, writing the first era yourself, or running centuries of history), and on wide screens the year scale and legend beside the map rather than below it. "Initialize from Lore" is now "Begin from the world's history", and "Refresh Lore events" is "Bring in new Compendium events".
  • The Chronicle's map places each event's name where it has room: names no longer run into each other or off the edge, long names wrap onto short lines instead of being cut with "…", and a name with no free room is left to its dot's tooltip and the list. The list beside the map wraps long titles in full, and its small print says "not yet accepted", "on the map" and "lasts into later eras" instead of engine words; "Persistent consequences" is now "Show lasting events from earlier eras".
