CartoVox 1.0.1

More landforms to stamp and to find, water you can shape in the city builder, and a quieter, steadier app.

The application was called Atlas Studio until version 0.8.2. CartoVox 1.0 and later are free downloads; Delta builds before it were shared with invited testers on the Discord server. These notes are also readable offline inside the app, under Settings › Release notes. How to use the app is in the guide.

Highlights

CartoVox 1.0.1, dated 25 November 2026, is the first update to CartoVox 1.0. On a Mac or Windows, an installed 1.0 updates itself: choose the update on the notice beside the version on the title screen, or in Settings › Updates & support. On Linux, the notice leads to the new installer, also in the download section. Saved worlds show the new landforms when you open them. The full account is in the release notes.

  • Seventeen more landforms to stamp: the Sculptor’s Stamp adds a cenote, a sinkhole field, a blue hole, karst towers, a tepui, an inselberg, a canyon, badlands, sea stacks, a tombolo, a maar, a cinder cone, drumlins, an esker, a moraine, a salt flat and a crater lake. The list is grouped by kind, and each landform is described under it with the size it usually has. Choosing one sets the Height to suit it, and on a continent or region map the Size too. A stamped cenote, maar or crater lake keeps its bowl, so the rain can fill it. Sculpt.
  • Worlds find more landforms: cenotes, tower karst, blue holes, tepuis, inselbergs, mesa country, badlands, canyons, salt flats, sea stacks and arches, tombolos, crater lakes, maars and mud volcanoes, and the moraines, drumlins, eskers and cirques the ice age carved, each where its rock, climate, water and ice put it, a handful per world. They show on the Volcanoes, Karst, Coastal landforms and Peaks, waterfalls & oases maps with their own symbols and key entries, in the World panel, Find places and the GeoJSON export, and on continent and region maps. Canyons take their river’s name, and a landform you stamp is listed as yours.
  • Fill water in the city builder: the Terrain tool’s Fill water turns everything at or below a sea level you choose into sea, where it reaches the edge of the sheet, and leaves every island above it standing; or click a hollow and choose a height to flood it as a lake, pond, salt flat or sea. An outline shows what will fill as you move the height, following an imported heightmap and any hills you drew, so islands no longer need tracing by hand. City builder.
  • Water and land you can change: what you made with Cut water, Fill water, a river or canal, or Add land can be chosen again, on the map or from the Inspector’s list of ground edits, to change its kind and its height or width, move it or delete it.
  • Towns on small islands: a town on a small island you drew or filled now fills the land it has instead of coming out empty. When an island is too small for the people you asked for, the builder places as many as fit and says how many. Islands in drawn or filled water no longer trail a pale streak of shore.
  • Your drawing is what gets planned: ground you draw is what Generate city plans, even when you opened the builder from a world’s settlement. The heightmap import turns away a picture over 10,000 pixels on a side instead of risking the window, and the Inspector has a close button, a handle to bring it back and the I key.
  • Polyhedral and butterfly maps: on the Polyhedral (icosahedron) and the Cahill and Waterman butterfly World maps, rivers, roads, borders, coasts, journeys and your own drawn lines stop at the edge of each triangle or wing and carry on from the next, instead of running across the empty paper. Filled areas stop at each edge, names move onto one triangle or wing and read the right way up, and large, vector and sharpened exports follow the same rules.
  • Disc, region and flat worlds: they show the world taking shape while it builds, as a globe does. Their time and memory estimates are closer before you build (a 2,000 km disc at Ultra said about 3 minutes on a fast Mac and now says about 17), and finer detail never claims to need less memory. A disc or region you chose in Create is kept when you leave or reload, and the Library shows their whole map, round or uncropped, in the natural colours the build ended on.
  • A quieter, steadier app: CartoVox still gets your last world ready as it starts, but no longer shows its progress while you are elsewhere, and the title bar reads just “CartoVox 1.0.1”. On Windows and Linux, Safe graphics is now a switch in Settings › Updates & support, with Restart now, in place of starting with --safe-graphics. On Linux, if the window goes dark, CartoVox reopens the view by itself and, the second time in a few minutes, offers Safe graphics. The close button always closes the window.
  • Update notices: when a new version is out, the title screen says so beside the version, with a button to update, download or restart and a link to what is new. A dot on Settings points to it, and the command palette offers the update. Skipping a version is never final: Settings keeps saying which one you skipped, with Install it anyway and Stop skipping.
  • And dozens of fixes: Find places and Saved views light up only while their panel is open, Library counts are grouped like the areas beside them, map layers are named in your interface language, and a build’s Details say where its memory is.

Full notes

CartoVox 1.0.1 follows CartoVox 1.0, released on 22 November 2026.

Create

  • A disc world, a region world and a flat world now show the world taking shape while it builds, as a globe does. A disc or region opens on the card you chose, then fills in tile by tile as its ground is built, then turns to its natural colours; a disc is shown whole and round. A flat world shows its relief, rain and biomes on its own rectangle.
  • The time a disc or region world says it will take before you build it is closer to what it takes. It now counts what its tiles cost today, the joining and checking after them, and settling its peoples. A 2,000 km disc at Ultra said about 3 minutes on a fast Mac and now says about 17.
  • The memory a disc or region world says it needs before you build it no longer drops when you choose more detail: a 3,000 km disc said 10.4 GB at Standard but 9.6 GB at High and Ultra. A finer level now never says less than a coarser one, and a larger disc never less than a smaller one.
  • A disc or region you made in Create is kept when you leave Create or reload the app. Coming back shows the same disc, or the same places, and the one you had chosen, instead of building a new hidden planet for it.

Editing a built world

  • The Sculptor's Stamp has seventeen more landforms: a cenote, a sinkhole field, a blue hole, karst towers, a tepui, an inselberg, a canyon, badlands, sea stacks, a tombolo, a maar, a cinder cone, drumlins, an esker, a moraine, a salt flat and a crater lake. The list is grouped (Volcanic, Impacts, Mountains and valleys, Karst and caves, Coast and sea, Ice, Desert and dry land), and the landform you choose is described under it with the size it usually has. Choosing one sets the Height to suit it, and on a continent or region map the Size too. A stamped cenote, maar or crater lake keeps its bowl through the rebuild, so the rain can fill it. Every new landform works on the World map, continent maps and region maps alike.

Settings and the app

  • The window's title bar reads just "CartoVox" and its version, such as "CartoVox 1.0.1", with no world name, workspace or build progress. The Task Manager entry and the app's name read "CartoVox".
  • On Linux, when the window's display stops (it went dark, most often while importing a heightmap in the city builder), CartoVox reopens the view by itself and says so. If it happens twice in a few minutes, it offers a Turn on Safe graphics button.
  • Safe graphics is now a switch in Settings, under Updates & support, on Windows and Linux. It draws the window without the graphics processor: slower, but steadier on some graphics drivers. It takes effect the next time CartoVox starts, and a Restart now button starts it again for you. Before, it meant starting CartoVox with --safe-graphics. On Windows, the question CartoVox asks after a start whose window did not open now says where to turn it off again.
  • The window's close button always closes CartoVox, even when the window's display has stopped. It still asks first when a world is being built.

Cities

  • The City builder's right-hand Inspector panel now has a close button in its header, a reopen handle on the map's right edge, and the I keyboard shortcut; whether it is hidden or open is remembered between sessions, and the map key shifts clear of the panel so it stays visible. The redundant "City builder" tooltip over the page title is gone.
  • Ground you draw in the city builder is now what Generate city plans, even when you opened the builder from a world's settlement. Before, it planned the settlement's own ground and set your drawing aside, so the map seemed to go back to how it was. If you choose "A settlement in a world" again while drawing, the panel says your drawing will be set aside.
  • The city builder's heightmap import reads a picture straight at the small size it keeps, and turns away a picture over 10,000 pixels on either side instead of risking the window's display. Its messages appear right under the picture picker, with the picture's size written the way your language writes numbers, and a picture it turns away is cleared from the picker.
  • On a phone-sized window with the Inspector open, the city builder's map key stays whole on the map instead of running off its left edge, and the compass no longer sits on the city's name.
  • The city builder's left-hand rail no longer splits words such as "Stadtplaner" or "Szenenstudio" across two lines: it widens to fit the longest word, in every language.
  • In Polish, the city builder's notes about planning on your drawing now speak of what was drawn, not of what you will draw.
  • The city builder's Terrain tool has Fill water. Choose a sea level and everything at or below it that reaches the edge of the sheet becomes sea, with every island above it left standing; or click a hollow and choose a height to flood that basin as a lake, pond, salt flat or sea, stopping at higher ground. An outline shows what will fill as you move the height slider, and it follows an imported heightmap and any hills you drew first, so islands no longer need tracing by hand. Specks under 1,000 m² are left out, and the Inspector can raise or lower a fill afterwards.
  • Islands in drawn or filled water no longer show a pale streak of shore running from the island out to the open water.
  • A town generated on a small island you drew or filled now fills the land it has. Before, a small island often got no town at all: 1,000 people on an island 1 km across, or 3,000 on one 800 m across, came out as an empty map. When an island truly is too small for the people asked for, the builder places as many as fit and says how many; if no one fits, it says so.
  • On a phone, the handle that brings back the city builder's Inspector sits at the middle of the map's edge, and the map key, compass and city name keep clear of it.
  • Water and ground you drew in the city builder can be selected again. With the Select tool, click inside a lake, sea, pond, salt flat or filled water, on a river or canal, or on land and hills you added, and the Inspector shows it: make a lake a pond, widen a river, drag it somewhere else, reshape it by its points, or delete it (Delete or Backspace), each change undoable. The Inspector also lists everything you drew under "Ground you drew", so a small pond or land under a district can be picked from there. Works before and after the city is generated.
  • Drawing the ground first after planning a very large city no longer turns the window dark. The blank sheet for a metropolis is now farmed in fewer, larger fields, and the city builder lets go of the last plan's memory before drawing, so the page uses about half the memory it did. On a computer with little memory, the city builder asks before planning a city that may be too big for it.

Speed and memory

  • A build's Details say where its memory is: the helper processes a big build starts are counted as the build's, not the window's, and a small window reads in megabytes. An Ultra disc on Windows used to read "window 6.0 GB" while the window itself used 21 MB.
  • The memory line in a build's Details, how much is in use, by how many processes, how much is kept for reuse and how much is free, reads in your interface language.

Maps and exports

  • In a region world's Atlas, a division or town plate you pick while the world is still opening stays on screen. It used to jump back to the whole region when the world finished opening, leaving the plate you chose half drawn.
  • On the Polyhedral (icosahedron) and the Cahill and Waterman butterfly World maps, rivers, roads, sea lanes, walls, borders, coasts, currents and winds, the map grid, journeys, storm tracks, trade routes, measurements, sketches and your own drawn lines now stop at the edge of each triangle or wing and carry on from the next, instead of running as straight strokes across the empty paper between them. Filled areas (realm tints, faiths and peoples, hotspot areas, Chronicle realms and events, campaign fog, drawn regions) are filled up to each edge and never stretched across a gap. Names that would sit across a gap move onto one triangle or wing nearby, or are left out, and names on a turned wing now read the right way up. Large map exports, vector exports and sharpened views follow the same rules.
  • The Find places and Saved views buttons on the map stay lit only while their panel is open, and go out when you close it with ×, Esc or the button itself. Keyboard focus on the map's buttons has its own ring, so it no longer looks like an open panel.
  • On the Polyhedral World map, pole up or turned for fewest cuts, a pin, label, town or note lying exactly on an edge between two triangles (the date line, a fold meridian, a pole) now sits on the same triangle in the live map as in the map tiles and exports.
  • Worlds now find and name many more landforms: cenotes (water-filled sinkholes, now apart from the dry sinkhole fields), tower karst, blue holes, tepuis, inselbergs, mesa country, badlands, canyons, salt flats, sea stacks, sea arches, tombolos, crater lakes, maars and mud volcanoes, and the land the ice age carved: moraines, drumlins, eskers and cirques. Each appears where its rock, climate, water and ice put it, a handful per world, on the Volcanoes, Karst, Coastal landforms and Peaks, waterfalls & oases maps with their own symbols and key entries, in the World panel, Find places and the GeoJSON export, and on continent and region maps. Canyons take their river's name. A landform of one of these kinds you set down with a Sculpt stamp is listed and named as yours. Saved worlds show them when opened.
  • Clicking Find places or Saved views again while its panel is open closes the panel, as × and Esc do.
  • On a disc or region world's own map, Find places has a × to close it, and its search box asks for "A name on this disc" or "A name in this world" instead of a name in this region.
  • The View menu's Borders and places list and the Landmarks panel name their layers in your interface language, the levels of a world's divisions included: German reads "Ebene 2: …" rather than "Level 2: … and the like".
  • The installed app draws a place's climate chart and saves printed PDF sheets again. CartoVox 1.0 left out two of its chart formats, so on Linux a place almanac could fail to draw.

Saving, updating and the Library

  • When a new CartoVox is out, the title screen says so beside the version, with a button to update (or to download it, or to restart once it is ready) and a link to what's new. A dot on every Settings button and on Updates & support points to it, Settings opens straight on the update, the command palette offers "Update CartoVox to …", and a notice asks you to restart once the download is ready. Later quiets the notices but keeps the dot.
  • Skipping a version is never final: Settings › Updates & support keeps saying which version you skipped, with Install it anyway and Stop skipping, the notice after Skip has Undo, the palette offers the skipped update, and Check for updates offers it again. A newer release shows as usual.
  • Disc, flat and region worlds now show their whole map, centred and uncropped, in the Library's "Continue your world" panel, on Library cards and in the Map gallery.
  • The "Continue your world" panel is recomposed so the world's map fills the full height of the right side with the world's title, figures, actions and details stacked beside it. In a short window, such as a laptop's, the details stand beside the title so your other worlds still begin in the first view.
  • CartoVox still gets your last world ready in the background as it starts, but no longer shows its name, "Still opening" or the progress bar while you are in Create, Write, Make, Settings, the title screen or the Library. Choose that world while it is still getting ready and you see its progress there; if it is ready already, it opens at once. If it could not open, you hear about it only when you choose it.
  • The Library's Continue picture shows the world's card picture while its large picture is drawn, instead of a black frame for a few seconds the first time a world is shown.
  • Counts on Library cards and in "Continue your world", such as villages, towns, rivers and continents, are grouped the way the areas beside them are: "2 642 villages", not "2642 villages".
  • At phone width, the Map gallery's filters each take the room their choices need, so the Lettering filter shows "Named and no labels" whole.
  • A finished disc or region world's picture on its Library card, in the "Continue your world" panel and on the title screen is now in the same natural colours as the last picture you saw while it built, with its ice and forests, rather than in brown relief. A disc is round on the panel's own background, without the dark square and pale frame behind it.
  • The Map gallery now shows a disc, region or flat world's own whole map first on that world's shelf, not only its city plans.
  • A disc or region world whose map is still building no longer becomes the Library's "Continue your world" with no figures and an empty picture. Continue offers the last world you can open, and the one still building waits on its card, which says it is building.
  • The world switcher at the top of the window names the open world as soon as it opens, and says "Choose a world" from the first moment when you have worlds, instead of "No world open" for a second or two.
