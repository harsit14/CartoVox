CartoVox 0.8.3

Shape the world, then draw inside it

The application was called Atlas Studio until version 0.8.2. Builds are shared with invited testers on the Discord server; these notes are also readable offline inside the app.

Browse releases
All release notes

Version 0.8.3 includes every CartoVox change implemented after 0.8.2. The application has a new name and identity, while this release also adds a complete map-and-scene authoring system, world-derived settlement planning, reviewed world rebuild continuity, stronger manuscript interchange and focused world-generation improvements.

The application is now called CartoVox

  • New name and new artwork throughout: the window and browser-tab title, the header mark, the home logo, the installers, the desktop entry and every platform icon. The logo is supplied as two paintings, one for the dark theme and one for the parchment theme, because the plate behind the gold belongs to the drawing.
  • The dark theme is now called CartoVox Dark.
  • The portable Linux launcher is ./run-cartovox, renamed so the script and the CartoVox payload directory beside it cannot collide on a case-insensitive filesystem.
  • The Atlas tab, Atlas plates, Atlas lettering and the illustrated-atlas settings keep their names: those name the publication atlas the app draws, not the app.

Settings, saved files and data move to the new name

There is no automatic migration. Worlds and settings kept under the old name stay where they are and the new build will not read them; move them across by hand if you want them.

  • Worlds, settings, logs and update downloads now live under CartoVox in the per-user application-data directory, and under ~/.local/share/cartovox on Linux. The override variable is CARTOVOX_DATA_DIR.
  • Stored interface preferences use cartovox.* keys, so a browser profile that used the old build starts from defaults.
  • Existing signed beta access keys remain valid after the CartoVox rename. The verifier accepts both the established Atlas Studio audience and newly issued CartoVox keys while continuing to reject keys for any other product.
  • Project backups are project_backup.cartovox and coastline tracing drafts are .cartovox-trace.json. World recipes, art packs, tracing drafts, project backups and voxel samples declare cartovox-* formats, so files exported by 0.8.2 or earlier are not accepted.

Settlement files and links carry the CartoVox name

  • Saved settlement geometry is cartovox-settlement-scene, source packages are cartovox-settlement-source, and artwork skins are cartovox-settlement-skin.
  • Recipe links use a #cartovox= fragment. Older settlement links and files are refused with an explanation rather than being misread.
  • Generator metadata uses cartovox-native, feature identities begin cartovox:, and exported SVG identifiers use the same prefix.
  • Downloads use CartoVox names for settlement recipes, scenes, tiles, PNGs, rendered SVGs and artwork catalogs.

More natural generation and direct world drawing

  • Mercator is available as a live saved-world view as well as an export, with the same geographic data and explicit polar limits.
  • Mountain belts grow as connected systems of ranges with their own internal grain and irregular margins instead of reading as one repeated ridge.
  • Drawn cities keep the exact location chosen on the canvas. A drawn realm absorbs settlements inside its border rather than generating a competing capital over the same ground.
  • The terrain-brush size slider uses a ratio scale with a practical upper bound, giving precise control over small edits without sacrificing broad strokes.
  • Generated-world Tectonics and Weather tools now read their saved evidence reliably, and compact class panels fit without hiding controls or forcing horizontal scrolling.
  • Labels use plain geographic wording for the share of land in the largest connected region.

Manuscript import and formatting fidelity

  • Manuscript import recognises chapter structure across varied headings and applies size limits to the actual writing rather than a small set of form fields.
  • Italics and other emphasis survive manuscript import, world-to-manuscript transfers and supported exports instead of appearing as typed markup.
  • Imported notes become native manuscript notes. Word export emits real footnotes, while tracked changes deliver revised prose as readers see it.
  • Thesis vocabulary analysis reads the complete manuscript sheet rather than only the visible editor controls.

Villages and towns are planned street-first

Settlements are rebuilt from the ground up. The generator used to choose an outline and scatter buildings inside it, so every village came out as the same blob of unaligned boxes. It now lays the street network first, takes the blocks enclosed by those streets, divides each block into plots, and fits a building to each plot facing its street. The settlement's outline is whatever the finished blocks add up to.

  • Eight plans that differ in structure rather than in jitter: street village, green, crossroads, radial, organic, harbour, hilltop and twin neighbourhoods. The plan is chosen from the approach roads, water and relief a saved world already records.
  • Every plot faces a street, and every building on one side of a street sits on a shared frontage line, so a row reads as a row.
  • Quarters differ: market, green, craft, townhouse, gentry, manor, temple, dockside, poor quarter and crofts each set their own plot size, coverage, setback and building forms. Poor quarters deliberately do not line up, and are grown as one district.
  • The sheet is sized from the settlement instead of from the annual land budget. A village used to occupy under one percent of its own map. Fields are now drawn as a working belt around the town, with a depth control, and the hectares beyond the sheet are reported rather than drawn.
  • A 40,000-person city generates in about a tenth of the time it used to take.

Settlement artwork is drawn, not stretched

  • Roofs are built from each building's own footprint, with ridges, hips and courses, so an L-shaped or long narrow house gets a roof that fits it. Previously one of six drawings was squashed to whatever rectangle it was given.
  • The catalogue grows from 30 to 57 pieces: eleven tree species, windmills, watermills, granaries, forges, chapels, gatehouses, fountains and troughs, beehives, livestock pens, woodpiles, fences, shrines, stocks, boats, jetties, and vineyard, flax and fallow field treatments.
  • Settlement colours now match the application's own art directions. Illustrated adventure is the default, with engraved and tabletop alongside it.
  • Fixed: artwork packs exported from the shared library could not be imported into Render Studio, because the two used different format names.
  • Fixed: recolouring a wood changed its backdrop but left the canopy in the old palette.

Designing a settlement by hand

  • Steer the generator directly: street density, plot size, building coverage, irregularity, the share given to poor quarters, and how far the countryside reaches.
  • Choose artwork by looking at it. The flat list of every asset is replaced by a picker showing the drawings themselves, filtered to what the chosen feature can actually use, with search and an explicit automatic option.
  • Place features on the canvas and have them turn to face the nearest street, or scatter a copse of trees in one click that undoes as a single step.
  • Draw a quarter on the map and rebuild only that part. The streets and every building outside it stay exactly where they are.
  • The settlement form explains itself: it says which planner is active and why a control is unavailable, the scene name sits beside Save instead of inside the import panel, colour channels are named for what they paint, and an unsaved draft can be restored from the first screen rather than from a panel you cannot open yet.
