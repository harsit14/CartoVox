Worlds

A planet simulated from the rock up.

Describe a world in plain words or pick one from Discover. CartoVox moves the plates, raises the ranges, runs the winds and rain, routes the rivers and settles the peoples, then names it all in tongues of its own.

  • Globes, disc worlds, flat worlds and regions as worlds of their own, from a short brief, a drawing, an imported map or Deep Time.
  • Strange skies. Rings with real shadows, a second sun, tidal locking, backward spin, extreme tilt and giant moons. New in 1.1
  • Ask it why. Click any place to read the terrain, winds, rainfall and history that made it.
  • Your own life. Create species and biomes and choose how strictly ecosystems shape their ranges.
  • An Underworld under every world, with rivers, seas, tunnels and caverns traced back to their entrances. New in 1.1
  • Sculpt and redraw. Paint ranges, drag coasts, rivers and borders, then rebuild; names and your notes follow the places they belong to. New in 1.1

Build times

A Quick globe took about 30 seconds on the 1.0 reference Mac; Create estimates time and disk before every build.

No generative AI

Simulation and a language-aware naming system make the world. The same seed and settings rebuild the same geography.

Start from your map

Import an image or heightfield, trace a reference or draw coastlines, and CartoVox simulates the world around them.

What happens next

Change the world and watch it answer.

Worlds don’t stand still. Try a disaster, an outbreak or a great work of engineering, see what it touches, and keep it in the world’s history only if you want to.

  • Cataclysm Lab. Place an eruption, impact, earthquake or rising sea, scrub through it and see which towns are struck. Export GIF, WebP, MP4 or WebM. New in 1.1
  • Contagion map. Start a plague, heresy, invention or rumour in a town and follow it along real routes, day by day. New in 1.1
  • Canals and dams. Cut a shipping route between waters, flood a reservoir behind a dam and read the flow below it. New in 1.1
  • Chronicle. Eras, foundings, wars and migrations on a five-lane timeline, played back on the map and exported as video. New in 1.1
  • War animator. Set sides, marches, sieges and blockades and play them against the roads and sea lanes. New in 1.1

Maps and exports

Every map of one world, true at every zoom.

Over 90 maps come from the same simulation: relief, climate zones, currents, soils, realms, faiths, tongues, trade, hazards and more. Zoom from the globe to continents and regions built in finer detail, then make them beautiful.

  • Atlas plates you can edit: any stamp, frame and title, in styles from Woodcut print to Night atlas, printed as posters across pages.
  • Style packs. Browse looks on real previews and exchange a world’s look, ramps, symbols and fonts as one file. New in 1.1
  • Render Studio. Art directions, compass roses, borders and cartouches of your own design, and layered PSD files up to 8K. New in 1.1
  • Explore in 3D. Fly over a closer map or a built city, save viewpoints and export a flight up to 4K. New in 1.1
  • Print and make. Paper globe gores, interlocking relief tiles as STL and 3MF, cutaway block diagrams and schematic route maps. New in 1.1
  • Take it anywhere. SVG and PDF vector maps, GeoJSON, offline web maps and a World website with searchable dossiers. New in 1.1

Cities, scenes and dungeons

Towns, keeps and ships on the world’s own ground.

The city builder and Scene Studio read the land a settlement stands on: its river, coast, slope, climate and farms. Plan a village or a metropolis, then walk into its buildings deck by deck and floor by floor.

  • Cities from a village to two million people, grown in eras on blank ground or on a settlement in your world.
  • Above and below. Sewers, cisterns, catacombs and tunnels; split plots, change buildings and lock landmarks. New in 1.1
  • Street gazetteer. Search by street, address, keeper or trade and save it as Markdown or CSV. New in 1.1
  • Scene Studio. Ships, airships, keeps and dungeons with linked stairs, doors, room keys and working lights. New in 1.1
  • Into any game. Export walls, doors and lights for Foundry VTT, Fantasy Grounds Unity, Owlbear Rodeo and Roll20, or print a one-page dungeon. New in 1.1

Writing and lore

Write beside the map, with the world keeping score.

The Compendium and Chronicle start full, written from the simulation. The Manuscript keeps your book next to them, so every place name is a link and every rename can be reviewed across all your books.

  • Manuscript. Chapters and scenes, insights and continuity checks, beta-reader DOCX round trips and print-ready covers. New in 1.1
  • Renames that reach every book, shown in context, keeping capitals and possessives, with one undo. New in 1.1
  • Compendium. Realms, settlements, faiths, species, tongues and your own magic systems, pantheons and factions. New in 1.1
  • Tongues and names. Language families with their own sounds, scripts you can export as a font and names you can hear. New in 1.1
  • Dynasties and calendars. Family trees with succession laws, cultures’ calendars, festivals and natal skies. New in 1.1
  • World Bible as PDF or EPUB, and an Obsidian vault of linked notes.

The table

Run the game in the world you built.

Campaigns live inside the world: the party’s journeys use its roads and weather, scenes come from its towns and ships, and the story canvas keeps your clues pinned to real places.

  • Tabletop. Fog, sight, tokens, turns, dice and local ambience; players join from their own phone, tablet or computer.
  • Story canvas. Notes, dossiers, factions and live map crops joined by labelled arrows, with a player-safe view and slides. New in 1.1
  • Props Studio. Town broadsheets, charters, wanted posters, letters, cargo manifests, GM screens and standees. New in 1.1
  • Tokens. Transparent tokens and printable medallions from portraits, heraldry or creature silhouettes. New in 1.1
  • On the road. Travel with elevation, weather, goods and prices; hexcrawl maps with stamps that survive rebuilds.

Discover

Start from a world someone already loves.

Discover is a shelf of hand-picked recipes inside the app: 47 globes and nearly a hundred regions, discs, flat worlds, cities, ships, dungeons, scenes, tabletops and style packs. Each one builds on your own computer in a click.

  • Filter by kind, climate or rarity, from common continents to legendary ringed and tidally locked worlds. New in 1.1
  • Daily picks and themed collections, each marked with what it holds. New in 1.1
  • Share to Discover. Package a creation with four pictures and a credit as a .cvxshare file to send or offer for the catalogue. New in 1.1
  • Works offline, in all six interface languages. New in 1.1

Speed and accessibility

Quick to find, quick to use, open to everyone.

A big app should still feel small. Search reaches every tool and every named thing, a focus puts your kind of work first, and the whole studio works by keyboard and screen reader.

  • Search everything with Ctrl/⌘ K: tools, places, streets, shopkeepers, characters, events and chapters. New in 1.1
  • Workspace focus. Novelist, Game master, Cartographer or Worldbuilder puts the right tools first; combine two. New in 1.1
  • Six languages, with plurals and dates that follow the interface language.
  • Keyboard and screen reader access to the World map, tools, charts and tours; Reduce motion and large text respected.
  • Lighter and faster. Smaller installers, faster illustrated plates and leaner memory for large worlds. New in 1.1
  • Updates itself on macOS, Windows and Linux, checking each update against our signature.

Hardware

macOS 14+ on Apple Silicon, Windows 10/11 x64, Linux x64 with glibc 2.34+. 8 GB of memory at least, 16 GB recommended.

Disk

Quick worlds take roughly 120–240 MiB without illustrated plates; plates and exports add more. Create estimates your selection.

FAQ

Common questions.

Does CartoVox use generative AI?

No. Simulation and a language-aware naming system create the world. The same seed, settings and simulation version reproduce its physical geography; rendered pixels can vary.

Does it need an internet connection or an account?

No. There is no account, and CartoVox works offline. It only checks for new versions, which you can turn off. Your worlds and writing stay local. Privacy details →

Is CartoVox free?

Yes. It is free to download and use, with no invite, access code or account. Download →

When is CartoVox 1.1 out, and what do I get today?

Today’s download is CartoVox 1.0.1. Features marked “New in 1.1” arrive with the 1.1 update, which 1.0.1 offers you when it is released. Release notes →

Can I start from a map I already have?

Yes. Import an image or heightfield, draw your coastlines, or trace a reference. CartoVox simulates the world around them.

Can I sell what I make with it?

Yes. Your outputs are yours, with no royalties or required credit. The app itself may not be redistributed. Licence →

Is the simulation scientifically accurate?

Physically motivated, deliberately simplified. CartoVox is a worldbuilding model, not a forecasting tool. Known limits →

How big is a world on disk, and how long does it take?

It depends on your settings and computer. A Quick globe took about 30 seconds on the 1.0 reference Mac and roughly 120–240 MiB before plates; the app estimates each build before it starts. Reference measurements →

I tested a Delta build. What changes?

Install the current release; it needs no access code and sends no usage report. Your saved worlds and exports stay on your computer and open in it.

Where is the manual?

Open Help in the app for the offline manual, or use the website guide.

How do I get help or report a bug?

Join the Discord server; the app’s bug button leads there too.
