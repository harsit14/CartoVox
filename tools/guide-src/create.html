Create › Brief · step 1

Describe the world you want

Brief walks you through three steps: 1 Describe, 2 Candidates and 3 Build. First choose how to begin on the right: Generate a world, Import a map, Draw a world, A region as its own world, A disc world, A flat world or Begin with Deep Time.

The Describe step. The sections on the left refine the brief. The cards on the right choose how the world starts.

Type what matters under Your world in a few sentences: the climate, the coasts, the mountains, what the map must show. Which words does it know? lists the words the brief acts on, and after a build it reports any words it read but didn't use. Start from a genre, Use an example and Surprise me give you something to start from.

Planet sets the size, the tilt, the year and the moons. In 1.1 it can also add rings, a second sun or a planet locked to its star, which keeps one side in daylight {new}. Land sets how much land there is and its shape. Peoples and Tongues shape nations and their languages, Tectonics moves the plates and Limits sets hard bounds. Place your key features pins a mountain range, a desert or an inland sea to a spot on the globe.

Advanced holds every planet, climate, tectonic, water, civilisation, naming and output setting, with a search field. It also holds your own biomes and species rules. A set of choices travels as a short code: Copy sharing code on the Build step copies the seed, the description and every setting in one line.

Create › Brief · step 2

Compare candidates and choose one

Search candidates, then Find 12 strong matches, previews a batch of seeds against your brief. Nothing is saved yet. Click a candidate to choose it, or compare two to six side by side. Favourites and the reasons you give for rejecting a candidate steer later searches.

Keeps the seed and plate layout and tries small changes to the coasts and relief. Search again always starts fresh, and Recent searches keeps the last ten sets.

Create › Brief · step 3

Build at Sketch, Quick or Full detail

The Build step shows a large preview, the seed and the statistics, with time, memory and disk estimates for the exact choices you made. Pick a detail level and choose Create Sketch world (or Quick, or Full). Times measured on a fast Mac: about 15 seconds for Sketch, 30 for Quick and 80 for Full. Illustrated atlas plates add more. A coarse world can be rebuilt finer later, as a separate project.

Close the progress window and the build carries on. A dock shows the phase and the time left, even if you reload. Stop after this plate finishes the current image and stores the rest. An interrupted build can be resumed.

Keep the recipe and roll a new seed, or build the same world again at a different detail level. The new world records where it came from.

Create › Import/Draw

Bring your own map, or draw one

Bring in a painted map, a heightmap, GeoJSON, STL or OBJ. CartoVox reads the coastline and asks you to check it, then asks a few questions in plain words: how high the peaks reach, and how wet and warm the world is. You choose whose rivers the world gets, yours or the simulation's. Mountains and plate lines you mark on a guide page raise real ranges. In 1.1 an imported world keeps the name you gave it at Build {new}.

Paint the land on a blank canvas, and CartoVox simulates the climate, rivers, peoples and names. Drafts save as you go, with their reference pictures and undo history.

Other shapes

Regions, disc worlds and flat worlds

Region world builds one county, kingdom or continent at its true size, at Standard, High or Ultra detail (up to a 16,384-pixel map), with its own towns, roads and divisions. Disc world builds a flat, round world with an Edge. Flat world builds a rectangle or ellipse with its own sun, winds and seasons, and no planet behind it. In 1.1 flat worlds can be long and narrow, and region sites can be found even when the climate and latitude are left at Any {new}.

Create › Deep Time

Deep Time

Deep Time runs a supercontinent cycle over hundreds of millions of years. Sketch or generate the first landmass, run the eras, then keep the one you like as the start of a normal world. Play the history at four speeds, step through it with Earlier and Later, and draw it as climate, rock age, height or plates. In 1.1 it shows the starting coastlines before you run a cycle {new}.
