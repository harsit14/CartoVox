Delta V3 was shared with invited testers on 29 September 2026. Your access code keeps working and the licence is unchanged. It answers the loudest thing testers told us, that the app went quiet while it worked, and it brings new science: four kinds of wetland, warmer high latitudes, soils that are wet where water gathers, and continents that rift apart in Deep Time. Saved worlds change only when you next rebuild them. The full account is in the release notes.

  • The app says when it is working: the button that started an export, import, save, journey or render counts its seconds, the part of the screen it will fill dims, and anything slow keeps a notice up. Undo survives a reload in Render Studio, scenes, Draw your world, the Sculptor, the settlement editor, Deep Time's drawing and every chapter, and long dropdowns open a searchable list inside the window.
  • Shape while it builds: a Full world's build window opens a workbench over the relief that exists so far. Stage Raise, Lower, Plateau, Ridge and Island edits and story pins, inspect and measure, and hand the edits to the Sculptor when the world is saved. Create a world.
  • Pinpoint tin, silver and amethyst: the Deposits layer (World › Map appearance › Map aids › Deposits, key O) pins 25 minerals and gems where the world's own rock puts them, each with its grade, the geology that made it, the nearest town and, for worked sites, a name in the realm's own tongue. Search it, filter by metal, gem or worked site, and read the deposits beside any cell in the inspector. Map views.
  • Species maps with a key and filters: search species by name or use, filter by group, and read what every colour means beside the map. 69 species now, with saffron, cinnamon, pepper and the other spices, hops and barley, flax and hemp, honeybees and silkworms, and pearl oysters, coral, cod and whales in the sea. Map views.
  • Wines, ales, meads and 85 goods: what a town makes comes from what grows around it, the deposits beside it and its harbour, and towns are known for named specialties, a wine of the volcanic slopes or a forest mead, shown in the dossier, the Codex and the market editor. Codex.
  • Several measurements at once: finish a route or close a shape and it stays on the map in its own colour with its total, listed to hide, rename or delete, and remembered for each world. The map.
  • Hand-drawn maps and maps in several files: a pencil or ink map photographed on plain or ruled paper is read by its drawn coasts, with Swap regions to fix any region in one click; and Combine several map files… joins scans or sheets of one world, placing overlapping files by themselves. Import a map.
  • Deltas you can see: rivers that build a delta draw their distributaries on every everyday map, and the dossier and cell inspector name the delta. The map.
  • Imports that ask what they are for: rebuild a world from your map, keep your land and heights exactly, keep your picture as the map, or import the project's own places. Azgaar provinces, religions, markers and cultures come through, and Read the names on this map… finds the lettering on a picture and queues every name for review, on your own computer. Import a map.
  • The brief is taken at its word: drawn realms keep their borders and capitals, "thirty continents" gives close to thirty, zones are set apart, and every geography and planet choice explains itself under the pointer. The World Brief.
  • Render Studio paints: a Painting layer with pressure, texture, stamp scatter and Range and Forest brushes; a Painted stamp style beside Drawn; shear, a draggable layer tree, reusable clip masks, linked library structures, six reference images each in its own projection, and sheets of 5,000 objects that still answer in under 50 ms. Render Studio.
  • Refine a region: a child map can be re-simulated two, four or eight times finer, with the world's rivers flowing in and realms, roads and city plans redrawn on the finer ground. The View menu's Borders and places lays borders, provinces and towns over any map. The map.
  • Scenes in isometric: walls, doors, floors and stairs stand up as lit blocks, live beside the plan. Step inside a building furnishes any building floor by floor, and child scenes follow their parent's world, sun and darkness. Scenes.
  • Sculpting keeps up: a 110-stroke review that used to time out after 30 seconds now takes about two. Towns, landmarks and borders stay on the map while you sculpt and can be dragged to stage a move, overlays line up in every projection, and what you wrote about lakes, ranges and rivers follows them through a rebuild. Sculpt.
  • The science: Paint plates in Deep Time, and supercontinents now break up. Land ice follows the warmest season, so tundra and boreal forest grow near the poles. Wetlands come as bog and fen, marsh, swamp forest and salt marsh. A region brief can ask for white gypsum, red or black volcanic sand, and stream order carries through lakes. Deep Time · Simulation health.
  • Writing and lore: native Scrivener .scriv import, two editable panes side by side, Edit on every generated Codex (now the Compendium) entry with lore scoped to the world, regions or a point, provinces divided up to four levels deep, an edited Obsidian vault brought back, and lore imported from spreadsheets with attachments. Manuscript · Codex.
  • At the table: a player atlas drawn under the campaign's fog, printable token sheets, and characters revealed to players one by one. Towns on hills terrace up the slope, fountains are fed by conduits, and generated streets no longer run through buildings. Campaigns · Settlements.
  • New exports: a printable Globe STL, hollow or solid, whole or in halves; an offline map carrying full dossiers; and an illustrated hex map that paints every hex as a textured tile with its stamps, rivers and towns, sized so a VTT grid can match it. Everything that exports.
