Delta V12, dated 24 October 2026, comes with installers for Apple Silicon Macs, Windows x64 and Linux x64 (still glibc 2.34 or newer). Your access code carries over and the licence is unchanged (still version 2.3). Worlds made with Delta V11 open as they are, and the Library offers Update to this version for them: updating changes their climate, lakes and trenches, so some towns and names can move. The world as it was is kept in the Library as a copy of its own, named for the release that made it. The full account is in the release notes.

  • A climate more like Earth's: equatorial lowland interiors are rainforest again, because over the continents the tropical rain belt is broader than over the sea. Summer monsoons reach the edges of the subtropics and the seas beside them while the deserts' dry cores stay dry, and tropical highlands and plateaus get a believably wet climate instead of a desert's. Rain falling on parched ground partly evaporates before it lands, land gives back the water its soil and plants really hold, and ocean winds keep more of their summer strength. Explain this cell says when a place's rain comes from moisture recycled over land, or was not traced at all. Cell inspector.
  • Lakes the right size: lakes lose water as real lakes do, more in hot, dry, windy places, so desert lakes are smaller and some basins that used to overflow stay closed. Overflowing lakes wear down their outlets and shrink or drain, so worlds have far fewer giant lakes, while dry basins keep their salt lakes and salt pans. Rift valleys are chains of narrow basins divided by high ground, holding strings of long, narrow lakes the size of Tanganyika or Malawi rather than one small sea. Maps.
  • Trenches as troughs: ocean trenches are narrow troughs along the plate edge, with their volcanic islands on one side, instead of now and then opening into broad, deep basins; the deepest still sound to about 11 km. Advanced › Trench depth sets only how deep the deepest go, and 0 gives an ocean with no trenches, the rest of the sea floor unchanged. Advanced settings.
  • Built island and Wall: Sculpt's Built island turns a shape drawn in the sea or against a coast into a paved stone platform behind a sheer sea wall, 4 m high unless you choose otherwise. No river rises on it, nothing grows on it and the waves never wear it away; it has its own colour and key entry, is named in the Compendium, search and map labels, can hold a settlement, and can be moved, resized or removed from the Edits list. Wall draws a great wall, sea wall or earthwork across land, out over the sea or both, named on every map, atlas plate and export. Walls don't change travel yet. Sculpt.
  • Edits stay where you made them: an edit in one corner of the world no longer renames towns and capitals on the far side. On a whole rebuild, settlements stay where they were unless the ground under or around them really changed, and land added in the open sea no longer renames towns, realms, rivers and regions elsewhere. Every simulation-health warning has Show on map, and where a fix is known Preview repair shows it before anything changes. Simulation health.
  • Candidates for your own maps: imported and drawn maps have a Candidates step before Build. Choose from 6, 9 or 12 versions of your world, each keeping your coastline, guide layers, drawn places and answers while raising its mountains and rivers differently; compare them side by side and build the one you pick. If your map already fixes its own heights, the step says so and goes straight to Build. Import a map.
  • Real detail when you zoom in: the relief and biome maps zoom to 16× and say how far the saved cells are magnified. Close in, Show real detail re-simulates the whole view, land and sea, in place at finer detail; tick Automatically to have it happen whenever the map comes to rest. Closer still, each town's generated plan appears at its true size and place, a capital about a kilometre across, down to street scale, and a town without a plan offers Draw this town's plan. The layer browser opens with Maps by question. Maps.
  • Studios with the map first: in Settlement Studio the plan fills the window, with its settings and tools in columns beside it; Scene Studio's canvas and Travel's route map come first too. Settlement Studio and Scene Studio zoom about the pointer and have Fullscreen (F, and Esc to leave), and Travel has zoom, Fit and Fullscreen on the map's own bar. A town saved as a scene measures its true width on the tabletop, Travel offers every settlement and adds up a sea journey door to door, and the scene library opens on your saved scenes, newest first. Settlement Studio · Scenes · Travel.
  • Create shows what it carries over: nations, tongues, placed areas and a tectonic plan from an earlier session are listed on Describe, each with Remove, and Build asks once before including them. Reset all is now New brief, which can be undone while the page is open. Before you build, the Build step shows how your brief resolves: what will be used, which earlier choices beat your description, and what will not be applied, each with its fix. A world can now be the moon of a giant planet, under Star, orbit & moon. The brief.
  • Measured again: on the reference M5 Pro Mac a Sketch world took 30 seconds, Quick 57 seconds and Full 193 seconds, against 30, 53 and 174 seconds in Delta V11: the new climate, lakes and rifts add to a Full build. These are source runs on the release code from 24 October 2026, not timings of the packaged installers. Build.
