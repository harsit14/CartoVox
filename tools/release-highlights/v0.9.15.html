Delta V14, dated 5 November 2026, comes with installers for Apple Silicon Macs, Windows x64 and Linux x64 (still glibc 2.34 or newer). Your access code carries over and the licence is unchanged (still version 2.3). Worlds made with Delta V13 open as they are, and the Library offers Update to this version for them: their resource maps gain the modern deposits, while towns, realms and names stay where they were. An imported world may also get new heights, rivers and lakes, so some of its towns and names can move, and a described area that asks for mountains or hills has its range built again by the plates. The world as it was is kept in the Library as a copy of its own. Region maps built with Delta V13 read as out of date until you generate them again, and a region world builds its region again as it opens; continent maps stay current until their world is updated. The full account is in the release notes.

  • Mark your mountains in Create: Shape of the land has a folded Mark your mountains. It opens a blank sheet of the whole planet, with the chosen land shape’s sample coast drawn faintly as a guide, where you paint belts of hills, mountains or high peaks at the width you choose, with Undo and Clear marks. Every candidate is built with mountains along your marks, worlds whose land lies under them rank ahead of worlds that leave them at sea, and each card shows your belts over its map and how much of them falls on land. The world you build raises its ranges there and keeps your marks in the Sculptor as its Mountain guide edit. With nothing marked, Create builds the same worlds as before. Create.
  • A mountain guide for imported maps: the import’s guide page has a Mountain guide. Add a transparent picture with the mountains painted in (or dark marks on a light one), or press Paint the mountains and paint over your own coastline, light strokes for hills, mid for mountains and dark for high peaks. CartoVox builds its own kind of ranges along your marks, with parallel ridges, foothills and valleys that rivers, ice and weather wear and that cast rain shadows, and keeps its other ranges low unless you tick Let CartoVox raise its own ranges elsewhere too. On a test coastline about nine tenths of the marked land stood above 1,500 m, against a sixth before. Your coastline never moves, and marks over the sea raise nothing. Import a map.
  • The Sculptor’s Mountain guide: paint where mountains go on any world you create, as Hills, Mountains or High peaks at a width you choose, and Apply rebuilds the world from its plates with real ranges along your marks. All your strokes are one edit, Undo takes back the last stroke, and after the rebuild the tool says how much of the marked land stands above 1,500 m and names any stretch it could not honour, and why: over the sea, worn down, or too narrow for the world’s grid. A described region that asks for mountains, highlands, peaks or hills is now built the same way, as a real range instead of a raised dome, and its ground-change strength sets how high it rises. Sculpt.
  • Plates that move as you say: under an imported plate map’s card, Plate motion shows each plate with a numbered dot; drag out of a dot to draw where it drifts, the arrow’s length giving its speed in centimetres a year, or pick Slow, Typical or Fast (2, 5 and 10 cm a year) or Reverse. A plate without an arrow keeps a simulated motion, so a map with no arrows builds as before. The page says in words what your arrows do along each shared edge and warns when two plates pull apart where your map draws mountains. On a built world, the Sculptor’s Plate motion tool shows every plate’s arrow to drag or set the same way; applying it rebuilds the world from its plates, so ranges rise where plates now meet, rifts and ridges open where they part and the old motion’s ranges wear away, while your own sculpting, kept areas, towns and roads hold as in any rebuild. Import a map · Sculpt.
  • Regions from menus: a region no longer needs a description. Its first step is a set of menus for climate, wetness, terrain, coast, water, who rules it, latitude band and the planet’s look, each starting on Any (Earth-like for the planet), and Find places with nothing chosen looks for good land anywhere. Your own words go in a folded Add your own words box; where a menu and your words disagree the menu wins, and a short note says so, as it does when two menus contradict each other (“Water: Fjords needs a coast, so Coast: Inland is read as Coastal”). The sheet remembers your choices, and the place cards’ checks list what you picked. The Polity menu crowns a Kingdom, Duchy, Republic, Free cities or Tribes. Descriptions also understand hills, mixed terrain, moderate rain, lakes and “island”. Create.
  • Truly wild regions: a region with Polity set to None/wild has no realm, even where a realm of its planet covers the place, and no baronies or shires. None of the planet’s towns stand in it, only at most a few scattered hamlets of herders and hunters joined by tracks. Its overview, Library card, gazetteer, atlas and Travel all say it is wild land, and Travel plans journeys between its hamlets or says plainly when there is nowhere to travel between. Travel.
  • A world on an icosahedron: the Polyhedral (icosahedron) projection lays the world on twenty triangles, five round each pole and ten round the equator, the classic hex-map world sheet of tabletop role-playing games, with every face keeping areas true. Choose it on the World map, where every look and overlay follows it, for atlas plates or as an Export picture, and turn it Pole up or Fewest cuts, which places the cut edges across as little of your land as possible. Export › Polyhedral world map draws the sheet ready to print: a hex grid on every triangle (5 to 30 hexes to an edge, numbered if you like), the climate bands named at your world’s own tilt, the names of continents, realms, seas and towns, a title and glue tabs to cut it out and fold it into a globe, as a PNG up to 8,192 pixels wide or an SVG or PDF. Export › Large map now works for the whole world too, in any projection, up to 16K. Map views · Export.
  • Modern deposits: the Resource prospectivity map shows uranium, lithium (brine under dry salt flats and old pegmatite), rare earths, bauxite, phosphate, diamonds, and offshore oil and gas on the shallow shelf, each with its own colour, shown alone or together, on the world map and on continent and region maps. Each follows the rock that makes it, from uranium in granite to diamonds only on the oldest cratons. The Deposits pins add uranium mines, lithium fields, rare-earth and bauxite mines and geothermal fields, a place’s dossier gives a line for each prospect under it, and the new classes and pins go out in the SVG, PDF, GeoJSON and KML exports. Towns, realms and names do not move. Map views.
  • Imports read better: a whole-world map keeps polar land along its top or bottom edge instead of mistaking it for a ruler strip, and a plain grey or light frame round a map is cut away instead of being read as sea. An imported coastline over what would have been open sea now stands on rolling hills and basins that rise inland, so rivers branch and wind to the sea instead of running in ruled lines. A mountain guide stays with the world through detail upgrades, backups and saved copies. Import a map.
  • Memory given back, Full builds about a fifth faster: when a build, continent or region map, large export or Render Studio render finishes, and after about a minute with nothing to do, CartoVox lets go of what it kept for quick viewing. After a long session it now settles a few hundred megabytes above what it used at opening, where it kept over a gigabyte more, and you no longer have to quit and reopen it before a region map will start. The climate step now uses more cores inside each season, so a Full world builds about a fifth faster and comes out exactly the same, down to the last number; Low memory builds keep to one core. Build.
  • Measured again: on the reference M5 Pro Mac a Sketch world took 18.8 seconds, Quick 43.1 seconds and Full 130.4 seconds, against 19.0, 47.7 and 166.8 seconds in Delta V13, so Create now quotes about 20 seconds, 45 seconds and 2 minutes. Without the compiled climate kernel they took 20.6, 63.0 and 338.8 seconds, still quoted as about 20 seconds, 65 seconds and 5½ minutes. These are source runs on the release code from 5 November 2026, not timings of the packaged installers. Build.
  • Also: a Library card’s buttons wrap onto a second row instead of pushing Delete past the card’s edge, and the Continue band lays itself out by its own width, so a world’s name and details no longer squeeze into a sliver beside the map. Library.
