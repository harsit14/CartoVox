Delta V11, dated 23 October 2026, comes with installers for Apple Silicon Macs, Windows x64 and Linux x64 (still glibc 2.34 or newer). Your access code carries over and the licence is unchanged (still version 2.3). Worlds made with Delta V10 open as they are, and the Library offers Update to this version for them: updating reshapes their coasts, so the rivers, towns and names along them can change, and natural regions can be renamed. The world as it was stays to go back to. The full account is in the release notes.

  • Coasts the sea has shaped: on every new world, glaciers of the last cold age carve fjords into high-latitude mountain coasts, the sea's rise since then drowns big river valleys into long inlets and funnel-shaped estuaries, and waves hollow soft rock into bays between hard headlands, leaving capes, cliffs and sea stacks. The sea builds coasts too: barrier islands with lagoons behind them, spits, and in warm, clear, shallow seas reefs, sand cays and atolls. Advanced has Coastal erosion (0 keeps the coasts as the plates drew them), Glacial fjord depth and Coastal deposition. A Full-grid cell is about 27 km across, so smaller fjords, barriers and atolls are drawn only in child maps and the HD region. Imported and drawn coastlines stay exactly as you made them. Advanced settings.
  • The Coast Lab (World › Tools, beside the Erosion Lab) shows what the sea did: the sea floor in depth with shelf contours, the old shoreline against the new, what was cut away and raised, and a cross-section of the ground wherever you click a coast. Lower the sea by up to 120 m to see the shelf an ice age would bare. Try settings compares another strength first; nothing changes until Apply to this world, which asks first and keeps the world as it was as a restore point. The Climate Lab can now apply its changes the same way. Labs.
  • Land below sea level and desert oases: where two continents slide past each other and pull slightly apart, a few long, narrow basins sink below the sea, as at the Dead Sea and Death Valley, holding salt flats or salty lakes in a dry climate. They have a deeper green of their own on every map and are named landmarks ("the ... Depression"). Oases rise from old groundwater in the lowest ground of deserts on sandstone and limestone, desert towns gather at them, and a town at an oasis has a spring in its plan. Maps · Cell inspector.
  • Nation strength: every nation in the Nations panel, and each realm on Create's roster, has three sliders. Strength decides how much ground it takes from its neighbours, naval reach how far across the sea it claims islands and coasts, and natural borders how firmly mountains, great rivers and deserts stop it. The ground it would claim is previewed on the map before you rebuild; the preview is approximate. Small islands just off a nation's coast now join it, and adding a nation gives the world one more instead of replacing the weakest. Nations.
  • Bring your own rivers: the new Rivers and lakes guide layer reads a picture of the rivers and lakes you drew beside your coastline map, shows what it read, rings in red any river that reaches no other river, lake or coast, and lets you choose Only my rivers or Mine plus generated tributaries. Rivers on imported and drawn maps now run the way you drew them, each in its own valley down to the coast. Plate maps need not be perfect, and under The rivers on this map, Keep all water as water now comes first and is recommended whenever the reading is unsure. Import a map.
  • Create reads you better: What your description asked for says what became of every sentence, from checked by the search to not understood. Numbers are read in familiar units ("30% land", "nine major tectonic plates", miles, feet and °F), counts are read in context ("five continents, two of them large"), and every number it does not use is explained. Each suggestion has a checkbox, and Not what I meant dismisses a wrong one for good. Limits take exact ranges, and Story › Place your key features draws areas straight on the map. The description is still read in English whatever the interface language. The brief.
  • A calmer workspace: Sculpt stands in a column beside the map instead of over it, and Hide controls folds it to a slim rail of tool icons. Map panels, the cell inspector and the HD region card can be dragged and folded, map keys fold down to a small arrow, and World › Tools sorts its tools under four headings. Rename a world from its Library card, the Continue panel or the World overview, and Weather Year › Sky & planet shows the sky over any place. Panning and zooming the World map keeps up with the screen. Sculpt · Maps.
  • Names hold steadier: a new world's name is drawn from everything you chose and never repeats one already in your Library, or type your own in Name this world on the Build step. Natural regions are named last, from their own ground, so merging or splitting one renames nothing else, and drowned valleys and estuaries are named after their rivers.
  • Also new: Export › Rebuild map saves a whole world to build again; Terrain kit exports the ground for 3D software and printing; the Library shows an illustrated card for each world, and Archive to save space keeps a verified backup beside the world's card and moves the world to the Trash; Advanced › Recipes shares a recipe as a short code; and the time left on a build is worked out from this machine's own speed. Library · Exports.
  • Measured again: on the reference M5 Pro Mac a Sketch world took 30 seconds, Quick 53 seconds and Full 174 seconds, about the time they took in Delta V10 although the sea now shapes every coast. These are source runs on the release commit from 23 October 2026, not timings of the packaged installers. Build.
