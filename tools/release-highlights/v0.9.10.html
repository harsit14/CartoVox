Delta V9, dated 17 October 2026, comes with installers for Apple Silicon Macs, Windows x64 and Linux x64. Your access code carries over and the licence is unchanged (still version 2.3). Linux now needs glibc 2.34 or newer: Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 35, RHEL 9 or newer, or a current Arch. Ubuntu 20.04, Debian 11 and the RHEL 8 family are no longer supported, and the portable archive's name now ends in glibc2.34. Worlds made with Delta V8 open as they are, but the generator changed a great deal, so the Library offers Update to this version for every Delta V8 world. Updating or rebuilding can move biomes, rivers, lakes, towns, realm borders, names and lore; a world you edited by hand keeps your edits, and the rebuild reports what moved. The full account is in the release notes.

  • Rain falls where it does on Earth: the driest belt sits at 20–30° where Earth's great deserts are, the tropics and the storm tracks get Earth's rain, frozen seas no longer drizzle, and winds blow at Earth's speeds. Rivers carry about the share of the rain Earth's do (about a third of the land's rain reaches the sea), and in cold country they flood in spring, when snow melts on ground that is still frozen. Expect many more hot deserts and dry steppes in the subtropics, Mediterranean climates on west coasts and more boreal forest.
  • The soil carries rain from season to season, and the biomes read that water rather than the year's rain alone: a dry-summer Mediterranean coast grows open woodland, and a monsoon coast with the same rain grows closed forest. The cell inspector's Runoff & storage section shows the soil water each season.
  • Sculpting rebuilds only the part of the world your edit reaches: after a hill, a valley, a lake hollow or a built-over city, the rain, rivers, towns, roads, realms, names and lore away from the edit stay exactly as they were. The review says how much is rebuilt ("Rebuilds the area around your edit: about 3% of the world"), with Rebuild the whole world instead beside Apply. Edits that move a coast, change ice or a placed climate, or reach more than 15% of the land still rebuild everything. On a Quick world a small edit's Apply took 25 seconds instead of 93. Sculpt.
  • Ice domes, deltas, dunes and braided rivers: ice sheets rise as domes up to four kilometres thick, with the tallest mountains standing through them, and heightmaps and terrain exports show the top of the ice (a new Under the ice heightmap gives the ground beneath). Big rivers build deltas shaped by the river, the waves or the tides; deserts grow sand seas of crescent, linear and star dunes, shown on a new Sand seas & dunes map; and rivers with a strong flood season braid between gravel bars. All of it shows on every map and export.
  • Add or remove a river's branch with the River line tool: draw a new branch down to the river it joins, or click a tributary to take it away. Rivers you draw on the blank canvas or bring in from a map file are now river lines too, so they cut their own valleys and can be edited, renamed or deleted in the Sculptor afterwards. Sculpt · Draw your world.
  • German, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Polish almost everywhere: the welcome tour, the Library, Create, the map's dock and tools, the Terrain Sculptor, Render Studio, Write, the campaign table, tooltips and the error messages the app's server sends now follow the interface language you chose. Exports and saved files stay in English. Settings.
  • Faster builds, with measured costs: the released V9 reference is about 35 seconds for Sketch, 55 seconds for Quick, 3 minutes for Full without plates and 15 minutes with every publication plate. These are source-run measurements on an Apple M5 Pro, not timings of the packaged installers. The progress bar no longer sits at 17%, and Cancel works inside every season. A copy without the faster climate step, such as a Windows build made without a C compiler, takes longer, and its build log and estimates say so. Build.
  • Bring in your own map one decision at a time: Coastline, On the planet, Guide layers and Details are pages of their own, each guide layer says it arrived and how it was read, a Köppen climate map is read by the standard palette, and a tectonic plate map can guide an imported world's mountains. Polar, Albers, Lambert-93, World Mercator, Dutch RD New, British National Grid, Swiss and other national grids now import, and maps on older survey datums are shifted onto WGS 84. After drawing a map, Use my drawing as a guide keeps your coastline and ranges and builds natural ridges and valleys along them. Import a map · Draw your world.
  • A Nations panel lists every nation, generated or your own, to rename, recolour, describe, add by clicking where its capital stands, paint ground to, remove or merge; open it from Tools. A nation is the same colour on every map, and these changes rebuild only the peoples, so terrain, climate and rivers stay exactly as they are.
  • Create understands more: "mountains in the northwest, a desert to the east" becomes areas you can adjust on a small map, and every candidate card draws them; Limits set measured ranges, from continent counts to the highest peak, before you search; Find 24 variations tries small changes to the world you chose. Each continent now speaks its own languages, an Ocean divide can keep an old world and a new world out of contact, a planet can tilt past 90° as Uranus does, and a world can have permanent day and night. The World Brief · Candidates.
  • Cleaner maps and exports: picture exports no longer carry a grid you did not turn on, a Rivers button (key U) hides the rivers, and a downloaded map's key takes about an eighth of the sheet instead of over a quarter. A child map's hex map shows that map's own rivers, ground and towns with your pins, and a players' copy leaves out what players should not see. World facts lists every record and statistic of a world in one panel. Exports.
  • Also new: drawings and drafts are kept on disk, and CartoVox saves your work before it closes; new worlds are saved in folders named after them; saved versions take far less room; collect books into one omnibus and download Insights as a spreadsheet; plan sessions on a Campaign board, settle a clash with the battle simulator, and let players join a table from their own devices on your local network.
